# MSS v6 site: brand video concepts

*12 Oct 2026. Creative-director pass on Os's request: professional, created media on every page in place
of the janky screen recordings. Concepts only, nothing made. Companion to `media-plan.md`, which stays the
placement register: a concept here gets a row there once Os picks it.*

---

## 1. What the site does now

Screenshots taken at 1440px of Home, Website and How it works, 12 Oct.

**The pages are almost all text.** Home is about 5,900px tall and has two images: the hero beam (code) and
one UI still in the Problem section. Each product page has one image in the first screen, then around
3,000px of text. How it works has images on 2 of its 5 steps. About has the portrait only. Pricing, Start
and the home Proof, How, Founder and Start sections have none.

**The images we do have are screenshots shown too small.** Every one is a full browser window shrunk to
about 480px wide, so the client's site shows as a thumbnail with text you can't read. It reads as "here is a
screenshot", not "here is something beautiful we made". The original recordings had the same problem plus
real cursor wobble, no single focus point, and loops that cut mid-action. That is the jank: no one directed
these shots, they were just captured.

**The brand idea stops after the hero.** The lit stage (dark, one warm beam, the Spike tape marks, cues)
lives in the hero animation and the button hovers. Below the fold the site turns into a well-set editorial
text page. The identity has a whole theatre vocabulary (Soundcheck, Rehearsal, Your cue, Showtime, The run,
House Lights, Cue Red) that never gets shown, only named.

**What must not be lost:** the honesty layer. Real work, Live, Demo and Self-initiated badges, and no
mocked product, is a genuine difference from other studios. Rule 1 of the media plan ("product evidence is
never generated or mocked") stays. So the answer is not "replace the recordings with animations". It is two
separate layers:

| Layer | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand films** | Atmosphere and meaning: objects, light, the stage. No UI on screen, ever | Can be shot, generated or animated |
| **Product films** | Real captures of the real tools, re-staged by a director: big, slow, one moment each | Real footage only, demo data, badge stays |

## 2. Brief and insight

**Brief.** Owner-led businesses (clinics, studios, small regulated firms) judging whether MSS is
professional enough to trust with their brand and their systems. The site has to show craft, because the
site is the first thing MSS has made for them. Constraints: dark-first A identity, one Tungsten moment per
view, Cue Red for signals only, no stock people, no glossy AI portraits, no laptops on desks, no curtains or
theatre-mask clichés, motion as lighting cue, reduced-motion fallback. Higgsfield has 0 credits. The level is
an *advertising idea* (one thought every film carries), carried out as *execution* pieces per page.

**Tension (category).** Agencies sell polish, and the polish comes with someone else holding the keys.
For these owners, "professional" has come to mean "not mine".

**Insight.** *Owners want to look properly professional, but every polished thing they've bought came with
a lock on it, because the person who made it kept the controls.*

**HMW.** How might the films make polish and control feel like the same thing? (broad) How might every
film end with the owner's hand on the switch? (medium) How might the Spike mark, the empty spot, be filled
by the client's own work on screen? (narrow)

## 4. Scoring

Weights: originality .25, strategic fit .20, emotion .20, feasibility .15, scalability .10, simplicity .10.
Scored as a critic, not a fan.

| Concept | Orig | Fit | Emotion | Feas | Scale | Simple | **Score** | HumanKind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Standby / Go product films (4 + 5) | 7 | 9.5 | 7.5 (Tier 2: relief at being in control) | 8.5 | 9 | 9 | **8.2** | 7 |
| B. Ticket under the door (6) | 8 | 8 | 8 (Tier 3: rueful recognition) | 7 | 6 | 9 | **7.8** | 7 |
| C. Lit objects: ghost light, tally, house lights (7, 8, 12) | 7.5 | 7.5 | 8 (Tier 3: quiet reassurance) | 6.5 | 8 | 8 | **7.5** | 7 |
| D. One take (9) | 7.5 | 9 | 7 | 6 (needs Nafisa, live edit) | 5 | 9.5 | **7.4** | 7 |
| **System: A as spine, B, C and D as set pieces** | 8 | 9.5 | 8 | 7.5 | 9.5 | 8 | **8.4** | 7.5 |

**Why it isn't a 9.** It is a website media system, not a campaign. It wins on coherence and craft, not on
a single surprise anyone would share. The pattern is P08 craft as message (49 cases, saturated, so
originality is capped at 7 unless the craft is exceptional) mixed with P14 product as proof. The new angle
over the canon: in Honda's "Cog" the product does the work; here the *owner* does the last move in every
film. Two refinement passes (task unification, then the Oblique Strategy "use an old idea") took the system
from 7.9 to 8.4, then levelled off. Plateau, stated honestly.

**Kill your darlings: the case against.** Theatre metaphors can go twee fast. The theatre must live in
the craft (light, tape, cue lamps, hard shadow), never in the words over-explaining it. No curtains, no
masks, no velvet, no "break a leg". If a film needs a caption to explain the metaphor, it has failed.

**Pre-mortem: if this failed in six months, why?**

| Failure | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Brand films look like AI stock (glossy, too perfect) | High if generated | Shoot the objects for real, or generate with a hard brief: one practical light, film grain, deep black, no reflections |
| Product films still feel janky | Medium | Never shrink a full window. Capture at 2x, crop to the one moment, script the cursor on eased paths, hold the result 2s |
| Too much motion, the site gets heavy and slow | Medium | One film per view, muted, lazy-loaded, poster first, under 1.5MB each, static poster under reduced motion |
| Brand films crowd out the evidence | Low | Brand films only where there is no evidence slot; evidence keeps its badge |

## 5. The system: "Standby. Go."

**One sentence.** Every film is a single cue: the work is lit and waiting, the owner gives the go.

**Shared grammar (every film):**
- Opens on black. Tungsten fades up on one thing (the brand's signature move, now on film).
- One subject, one action, one result. 6 to 10s loop; the last frame matches the first so the loop
  doesn't jump.
- The Spike corners snap in once, on the result (the hero's stage tape, reused).
- Type is Archivo, set large on the frame, at most five words. Geist Mono for timestamps and labels.
- Cue Red appears only as a signal lamp (standby, on air). Never as decoration.
- 16:10 in the frame, a 4:5 cut for phones. Muted. No music on the site (a scored cut is a social
  extra).

**Three ways to make it, cheapest first:**
1. **Code motion (free).** Type, light, Spike marks, cue-light panels: HTML/CSS/canvas rendered to
   WebM/MP4 with Playwright and ffmpeg, the way `tools/clips/clip-kit.js` already works.
2. **Re-staged real captures (free).** Record the real tool at 2x on a large screen, then a director pass in
   the same code compositor: slow camera push onto the one moment, a scripted cursor, big type, the result
   held. This is what turns "screenshot" into "showcase" without faking anything.
3. **Lit objects (a shoot or credits).** Door, cue-light box, ghost light, tally lamp, microphone. Either a
   tabletop shoot (one lamp, black card, phone on a tripod at 4K, props about £60 to £120) or Higgsfield
   (0 credits now; each film needs a credit estimate first, per media-plan rule 6). Recommendation: shoot.
   Real light and real dust are the craft argument; generated objects risk the glossy look the brand bans.

## 7. Recommendation and order

Build the system, not a set of one-offs. Order by cost and proof:

1. **Website cue film, as the pilot** (free, demo data, no consent needed). Builds the shared grammar and
   the re-staging compositor once. If it doesn't look clearly better than the current still, stop and
   rethink before making more.
2. **Cue panel** (code, free) for home How and How it works 03.
3. **The other product cue films**: Campaign and Platform (Vera, free), then Content (Nafisa's photos),
   Identity recut.
4. **Object shoot**, one session: door and tickets, ghost light, tally lamp, cue box, mic, house lights.
   One evening with props and one lamp makes six films.
5. **One take** with Nafisa, last (live production, her time).

**Your calls before anything is made:**
- **Revive the hard-lit still life?** Brand identity Imagery item 3 says it was "dropped for launch, kept
  as a direction for later". The brand films are that direction, now moving. Yes or no.
- **Shoot or generate the objects?** Recommendation: shoot. Generating needs Higgsfield credits (0 now)
  and a credit estimate per film.
- **Confirm the two-layer rule:** brand films never show UI; product films are always real captures. That
  keeps media-plan rule 1 intact.
