# MSS site: hero film concepts

*13 Oct 2026. Creative-director pass on Os's request: a home hero film that shows the design thinking
behind each deliverable, because the site proves the systems but only the site itself proves the
creativity. Concepts only, nothing made. Companion to `brand-video-concepts.md` (12 Oct, the page-by-page
films) and `media-plan.md` (the placement register: the chosen concept gets a row there).*

---

## 1. Where the site is now

- **The hero is type only.** "Run the show." at up to 232px, bottom-aligned, with the code beam that finds
  the marked word. The 12 Oct concepts doc chose *no* hero video because "a video behind the hero competes
  with the type". That reason still holds for a video *behind* the type. It doesn't rule out a film *on the
  stage*, beside the type, in the empty black above the headline.
- **Every film made so far argues control.** The cue films (Identity, Website, Campaign, Content, Platform,
  Care, door, cues) all follow "Standby. Go.": the work is ready, the owner presses go. That is the systems
  story, and it's done well.
- **What's missing is the thinking.** A buyer sees the finished thing and never the decisions behind it:
  why this typeface, why this colour, what got cut. From the outside that reads as taste, or as something a
  template or a generator could do. It also makes the prices harder to justify.

## 2. Brief and insight

**Brief.** One hero film for the home page that shows the craft and the reasoning behind MSS's design,
across the products, without breaking the identity: dark first, one Tungsten moment per view, Cue Red for
signals only, Archivo and Geist Mono, no stock people, no glossy AI, no laptops on desks, no theatre
clichés, honest badges, reduced-motion fallback. Level: an *execution* of the existing "lit stage" idea,
not a new campaign platform.

**Tension.** Small business owners are offered two kinds of studio: design studios that make beautiful
things they can't run, and systems people who build things that work and look like software. Neither shows
its working.

**Insight.** *Owners can judge a finished design but can't see the decisions inside it, so they price it as
decoration. Show the decisions and the price makes sense.*

**HMW.** How might the hero show a decision being made, not just its result? (broad) How might each product
show where its design came from? (medium) How might the Spike mark, which frames the client's spot, become
the moment a choice is made? (narrow)

## 3. Ideation

Methods: SIT subtraction (take away everything but the decision), bisociation of a theatre prompt book (the
stage manager's annotated script) with design rationale, and reversal (show the rejects, not the hero shot).
Ideas 1 to 3 are warm-ups.

| # | Idea | Method | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Showreel: fast cuts of finished work to music | warm-up | Every studio has one, and it shows output, the exact thing that hides the thinking. Out |
| 2 | Screen timelapse of a design file being built | warm-up | Cliché, and it shows effort, not judgment. Out |
| 3 | Moodboard flythrough | warm-up | Everyone's moodboard looks the same. Out |
| 4 | **The cut.** Several real options lit on the stage; the light goes out on each reject until one is left, then the Spike corners snap round it | SIT subtraction | Kept: the decision beat |
| 5 | **Every choice has a source.** Each piece grows on screen from a real source: a painting's pixels become a palette, a photo of stage tape becomes the Spike mark, a messy photo library becomes a post | derivation, SIT task unification | Kept: the structure |
| 6 | **Prompt book.** Over the finished piece, Geist Mono notes draw in on leader lines, like blocking notes in a stage manager's script: "Teal taken from the paintings" | bisociation | Kept: one line per chapter, never more |
| 7 | **Same stage, different show.** The Spike corners stay fixed while each client's world takes the frame over in turn: her colour, Sable's black, PureMed's navy and gold | SIT, fixed frame | Kept as the rhythm: it proves there's no house style |
| 8 | **Relay.** Each chapter's result becomes the next chapter's source: the wordmark becomes the site header, the site's photo becomes a post, the post becomes a step in a journey | task unification | Kept: the "one studio" argument, in motion |
| 9 | Show the ugly first sketches | reversal | Partly kept: inside "the cut", only where the rejects are real |

## 4. Concepts scored

Weights: originality .25, strategic fit .20, emotion .20, feasibility .15, scalability .10, simplicity .10.
Scored as a critic, not a fan.

| Concept | Orig | Fit | Emotion | Feas | Scale | Simple | **Score** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. **Source to stage** (5 + 4 + 8 + 6) | 8 | 9.5 | 8 | 8 | 9 | 7 | **8.3** |
| B. Same stage, different show (7 alone) | 6.5 | 8.5 | 7 | 9 | 8 | 9.5 | **7.8** |
| C. Prompt book (6 alone, over static finished work) | 7.5 | 9 | 7 | 8.5 | 9 | 8 | **8.05** |
| **A refined:** four chapters not six, one decision per chapter, one mono line each | 8 | 9.5 | 8 | 8.5 | 9 | 8 | **8.5** |

**Why it isn't a 9.** It's a craft-as-message film (a heavily used pattern), so originality caps around 8
unless the execution is exceptional. What keeps it above the pattern: most process films show *effort*
(hours, sketches); this one shows *reasons*, and the relay shows design and systems as one continuous
piece of work, which is MSS's actual difference. One refinement pass (cut to four chapters) took it from
8.3 to 8.5, then it levelled off.

**Kill your darlings.** If the mono lines start explaining the metaphor ("like a stage manager..."), it has
failed. The lines state decisions, never theatre. And no "40 versions" claims unless 40 versions exist.

## 5. The recommendation: "Source to stage"

**One sentence.** Each chapter starts with a real source, tries real options, cuts to one, and hands its
result on to the next product.

**Grammar** (same as the cue films, so the site stays one system):
- Opens on black; the beam finds an empty Spike mark (the existing hero move).
- Each chapter: **source** (1s), **options** (1.5s), **the cut** (1s, lights out on the rejects), **lit
  result** with the Spike corners snapping in and one Geist Mono line (1.5s). About 5s.
- Each chapter's result travels to the next as a match cut (the wordmark slides up and becomes a header).
- Client work always in the client's own colours and type. MSS appears only as the light and the tape.
- Badges stay honest: Real work / Self-initiated / Demo shown as a small mono label on each chapter.

**Draft shot list (about 22s, loops back to the empty mark):**

| # | Product | Source (real) | Options (real) | The cut lands on | Mono line (draft) | Badge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | | Black stage, beam finds an empty Spike mark | | | | |
| 1 | Identity | Ayesha's painting in full colour; an eyedropper samples swatches out of it | Her three v3 directions (a, b, c) side by side, plus the font sheet | Her chosen wordmark and palette | `PALETTE FROM THE PAINTINGS, NOT A TREND BOARD` | Real work |
| 2 | Website | The wordmark slides up and becomes the header | Grid lines draw; the five rooms block in as tones | Her real homepage, settled | `FIVE ROOMS, ONE FOR EACH BODY OF WORK` | Real work |
| 3 | Content | Match cut on the Spike corners into PureMed's photo library, tiles lighting up | One photo tried at 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 crop frames | 4:5, set in her type, dropping into the grid | `CROPPED FOR THE FEED IT LIVES IN` | Real work |
| 4 | Campaign and Platform | A journey sketched as lines on House Lights: sign-up, guide, follow-up, booking | Each node fills in as its real designed email or screen | The full journey lit, one booking at the end | `DESIGNED LIKE A PAGE. BUILT LIKE A SYSTEM.` | Demo (Vera) |
| 5 | | All four results rest in a row and dim; the Spike corners close on empty space | | Loop | | |

Care is left out on purpose: it is keeping the show running, not a design decision, and the cue films
already cover it. Chapter 4 is the hinge of the whole film: it's where the design story becomes the systems
story, so the hero shows both halves of the business without saying so.

**Every mono line has to be true** and checked against the client's brief before rendering. Painting
titles and the real rejected options come from Ayesha's files, never invented.

## 6. Where it sits in the hero

The film doesn't go behind the headline. It goes on the stage in the empty black above it, framed by the
Spike corners, and the beam takes the one Tungsten moment from the word to the film after the intro.

```
DESKTOP 1440                                MOBILE 390
+----------------------------------------+  +----------------------+
| nav                                    |  | nav                  |
|                     ┌ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ┐  |  | Run the              |
|                       [ film 16:10 ]   |  | show.                |
|                     └ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ┘  |  | sub + buttons        |
|                     IDENTITY · REAL    |  | ┌ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ┐  |
| Run the show.                          |  |   [ film 4:5 ]       |
| sub line               [See] [Start]   |  | └ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ┘  |
+----------------------------------------+  +----------------------+
```

- Headline readable from the first frame (existing rule). The beam lands on the word, the tape snaps, then
  the light moves to the film and stays there.
- Muted, autoplay, loops, poster first, lazy after first paint, under 2.5MB for the 16:10 cut and 1.5MB
  for 4:5. Reduced motion: the poster is the lit result of chapter 1, no playback.
- A small pause control in Geist Mono (accessibility, and it keeps the film from feeling like an ad).

Rejected placements: full-bleed behind the type (competes, breaks one Tungsten moment); film first with the
headline arriving at the end (the headline would not be readable on the first frame).

## 7. Pre-mortem

| Failure | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Reads as a slideshow of screenshots | Medium | Never shrink a full window. Each beat is a crop of one detail at 2x, with eased camera moves made in the cue engine |
| Too busy for a hero | Medium | Four chapters, one decision each, one line each. If a viewer can't say what chapter 1 decided after one watch, cut more |
| Looks generated | Low | Every frame is real artwork or real UI, composited in code. Nothing generated |
| Shows Ayesha's unchosen directions before she's signed off | Medium | Ask her first (see calls below). Fallback: chapter 1 uses Sable (self-initiated) |
| Heavy hero, slow page | Medium | Poster first, lazy load, size caps above, preflight viewport pass |

## 8. How it gets made

All code motion in the existing cue pipeline (`tools/clips/cue/`, SOP-MSS-007): Playwright renders, ffmpeg
encodes, no credits. Pilot first: **chapter 1 only**, judged on Stage against the current hero. If it isn't
clearly better than the type-only hero, stop and rethink before making chapters 2 to 4.

## 9c. Version 2: the mark does the work (13 Oct 2026)

Os found version 1 too static and asked for something that morphs out of the logo and ends as the logo.
Scored against version 1 (7.5) and a pure logo morph (6.8), the hybrid scored 8.5: the Spike corners are
the one actor and never leave the screen; there are no cuts and no crossfades. Film:
`out-v6/hero-cue-v2.{mp4,webp}`, 21.4s, 1.1MB. Script: `cue/render-hero-v2.js` (same sources as v1).

- **Open:** the 40-grid mark at 200px. The corners pull apart and the painting is revealed inside them.
- **01:** the corners become a sampling frame. Each stop is the real pixel nearest a colour her site kit
  sampled, and the swatch drops out of the frame. Then a selection box crosses her five directions, each
  lit as it is framed, and opens to hold all five: her call.
- **02:** the box frames her logo, picks the retired spec, crosses to hers and keeps it, then moves to her
  homepage headline and opens out into the page.
- **03:** the box frames her words, then reshapes as a crop for each style: 4:5 Classic, Statement, then 1:1
  Signature.
- **04:** the House Lights page grows out of the frame (a wipe, never a grey crossfade). The box runs the
  journey and the rail draws behind it.
- **Close:** the page shrinks back into the frame, and the frame snaps into the mark, so the loop joins.

Same lines and badges as version 1. It ends on the mark, not the wordmark, because the headline and nav already
carry the name.
