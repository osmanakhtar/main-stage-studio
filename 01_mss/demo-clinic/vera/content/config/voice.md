# Vera Aesthetics — Social Voice Layer

**Canonical voice lives in `../../vera-tone-of-voice.md` (and `../../vera-brand-identity.md`). Read those first — this file only adds the social-specific layer on top. If anything here conflicts with the tone-of-voice file, that file wins.**

Brand character in one line: honest, calm, expert — the voice of a practitioner who would rather talk you out of a treatment than sell you one you don't need. Never hypes, never pressures, never chases a trend.

**Three words:** Honest. Calm. Expert.

## Social-specific rules

**Structure**
- First line is the hook, and it opens on the reader's worry, not the service. No throat-clearing ("At Vera we believe…"). They are nervous about looking "done" — meet that first.
- Short paragraphs, generous line breaks, real negative space. One idea per post. Restraint on the page mirrors restraint in the room.
- Exactly one CTA per post, at the end, and it is soft. Consultation-led: "Consultations are open", "Ask us before you decide", "If it's right for you, we'll tell you; if it isn't, we'll tell you that too." Never "Don't miss out" / urgency.
- British English throughout.

**Instagram captions**
- Hook within the first 125 characters (before truncation).
- 3–6 hashtags, at the end, on their own block. Considered, not a wall: mix category + intent, e.g. #NurseLedAesthetics #NaturalResults #SkinHealth #AestheticsDoneWell. Never #beauty-tier generic tags.
- Emoji: 0–2 per post, functional not decorative. The brand is calm; sparkle-spam breaks it.
- No link in caption (IG doesn't link) — CTA points to "link in bio".

**Humanise pass (mandatory before a post is marked drafted)**
Read every caption aloud once and fix anything that sounds like a language model or a brochure:
- **No em dashes (—), ever.** Use a comma, colon, or full stop. En dashes in ranges (3–6 months) are fine.
- No "isn't just X, it's Y" constructions, no "Here's the truth/thing" openers. Say the honest thing.
- No hype words: amazing, flawless, transformation, transformative. The brand promises natural results and keeps the promise.
- Vary sentence rhythm — not every paragraph ends on a short punchy fragment, and a list of three every time is a tell.
- Contractions everywhere a person would use them. Plain over clever; authority sounds like clarity, not jargon.
- If a sentence would survive on any clinic's account, cut or sharpen it until it's Vera's.
`content-lint.js` enforces the mechanical subset (em dash and POM brand names are blocking errors); the rest is judgement.

**Compliance is part of the voice, not a filter on top of it**
- Prescription-only treatments are never named. "Wrinkle-softening treatment", not the product.
- No before/after framed as a loud promise; results are shown quietly, with a "results vary / illustrative" framing.
- Every claim is one the clinic could stand behind. The voice and the compliance rules want the same thing: don't overpromise.

**Never**
- Price as the hook, discounts, flash offers, manufactured urgency.
- "Results guaranteed", "erase your wrinkles", transformation-promise language.
- Prescription-only medicine brand names (see compliance.md — hard rule).
- Clinical-cold lecturing without warmth, or trend-chasing to ride a search term.
- Vague wellness filler ("your natural beauty journey").

**Always**
- The reader is the subject ("your skin", "you'll notice"), not the treatment.
- Lead with the worry, explain rather than sell, and be honest enough to talk someone out of something.
- Ageing well, looking like yourself on a good day — never anti-ageing shame.
- Quiet confidence: a prescriber who has earned the authority to be plain.
