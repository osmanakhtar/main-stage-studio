## Character

The voice of a practitioner who would rather talk you out of a treatment than sell you one you don't need. Honest before it is anything else. Calm, because the work is careful and there is no rush. Expert, because a nurse prescriber has earned the authority to be plain.

It never hypes, never pressures, never chases a trend. It treats the reader as an adult making a considered decision about their own face. Warmth and expertise carry equal weight: the knowledge reassures, the honesty earns trust.

**Three words:** Honest. Calm. Expert.

## What it never sounds like

- **Salesy** — no "book now before it's gone," no manufactured urgency, no "treat yourself."
- **Hype** — no "amazing," "flawless," "transformation," no exclamation marks stacking up.
- **Clinical-cold** — expertise without warmth reads as a lecture. The care shows.
- **Trend-chasing** — no naming the treatment everyone's talking about to ride the search.
- **Overclaiming** — natural results is the promise, so the copy never writes a cheque the treatment can't cash.

## Principles

- **Lead with the reader's worry, not the service.** They are nervous about looking done. Meet that first.
- **Explain, don't sell.** Education is the whole strategy. A reader who understands the treatment trusts the clinic that taught them.
- **Say the honest thing.** "This won't fix that, and here's what will" builds more trust than any promise.
- **Plain over clever.** Authority sounds like clarity, not jargon.
- **Restraint on the page mirrors restraint in the room.** Short. Unhurried. Nothing shouted.

## Compliance is part of the voice, not a filter on top of it

- Prescription-only treatments are never named. "Wrinkle-softening treatment," not the product.
- No before/after framed as a loud promise; results are shown quietly, with consent, honestly.
- Every claim is one the clinic can stand behind. The voice and the compliance rules want the same thing: don't overpromise.

## The voice in practice

### Instagram bio
> Vera Aesthetics — nurse-led, natural results.
> We'd rather you looked like you, on a good day.
> Consultation-led. No pressure, ever.
> *A demonstration brand by Main Stage Studio.*

### Education post (myth-bust)
> "Will it make me look frozen?"
> Only if it's overdone. Done well, a wrinkle-softening treatment softens movement, it doesn't switch it off. You should still look surprised when your friend tells you she's pregnant.
> The difference isn't the treatment. It's the amount, the placement, and the practitioner deciding when to stop. That last one matters most.

### Practitioner / authority post
> People ask why the consultation takes so long.
> Because your face isn't a menu. Before anything is prescribed, we work out what's actually bothering you, what will and won't help, and what's better left alone. Sometimes the honest answer is "not yet," or "not this."
> A prescriber who never says no isn't being generous. They're being careless.

### Results post (compliant, quiet)
> Subtle is the point.
> No dramatic reveal here, just skin that looks a little more rested and a jaw that sits a little cleaner. Most people won't know what changed. They'll just think you look well.
> *Illustrative example. Individual results vary.*

### Offer / seasonal post (no hard sell)
> Skin-health season starts now.
> Before party season, a course of skin treatments does quiet work: better texture, more glow, nothing anyone can point to. Start in autumn and it's settled by December.
> If it's right for your skin, we'll tell you. If it isn't, we'll tell you that too. Consultations are open, link in bio.

### Patient-experience post
> First time is the nervous one. Here's exactly what happens.
> You sit down, we talk, nothing is decided in a hurry. No treatment on a first visit unless you're certain and it's right. You can leave with a plan and think about it for as long as you like.
> Nothing here is one-way.

## Quality check for Vera copy

- [ ] Does it open on the reader's worry, not the service?
- [ ] Is it honest enough to talk someone out of something?
- [ ] Have all hype words and exclamation stacks gone?
- [ ] Are prescription treatments unnamed, claims substantiated, results honest?
- [ ] Is it calm and unhurried, short where it can be?
- [ ] Read aloud, does it sound like a trusted practitioner, not a brochure?
