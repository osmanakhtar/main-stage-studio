01 — The Brief

What was
in the head.

Identity before exposure. A founder who knew what the music felt like but not yet what it looked like.

"Independent artist. Nothing released yet. Building around a specific sound — I know what it is, I just can't explain it to anyone."

"I don't want to look like anyone else in this space. Not the polished major-label version. Not the lo-fi bedroom aesthetic either. Something in between that doesn't exist yet."

"I need the world to exist before I put anything out. I want people to arrive somewhere, not just discover a song."

Songwriter and producer. Pre-release. No label. Building entirely independently with a clear sense of sound and almost no visual language to match it.

02 — Discovery

Before anything
looks like anything.

What kind of artist?

R&B artist. Soul singer.

Alternative pop.

Genre labels are distribution tools — they tell people where to find the music, not what it means. Starting with genre flattens the identity before it exists.

A songwriter building around a specific emotional register. Quiet, heavy, lit from within. The genre will follow the brand — not the other way around.

What does a brand need to do before a release?

Build awareness. Grow a following.

Awareness without context is noise. You don't want people to know you exist — you want them to know what you mean. Awareness before identity just creates confusion at scale.

Create the conditions for the music to mean something when it arrives. The brand is the room. Everything else is what happens in it.

What's the name and the world?

Stage name derived from birth name.

A colour. A feeling. Something abstract.

Both routes feel chosen. The name needs to feel inevitable — like it couldn't be anything else. A colour that isn't just a colour.

Sable.

Black with warmth in it. The name carries the palette, the register, the temperature of the music — all at once. The proposition follows: the signal before the noise.

Signal.

The proposition

"The signal
before the noise."

The thin line of warmth in the dark. Something there before you can fully hear it. Not a promise of what's coming — a presence that says something is already here.

03 — The Mark

One line.
Everything.

The mark is the proposition made visible. No symbol, no letterform — just the signal itself. A horizontal line of warm gold on deep charcoal.

The line — The signal

The single horizontal mark. Warm, present, not loud. The thing that's there before you fully register it.

The field — Sable

Not pure black. Deep charcoal with warmth in it. Black that's been lived in. The name in the palette.

Together — Identity before exposure

The brand is already present before a note is heard. That's the whole brief. Done.
