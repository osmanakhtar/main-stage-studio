# Sable — Tone of Voice

## Brand Character

Guarded. Present. The artist who gives everything in the music and almost nothing outside it. She's not withholding out of arrogance. She's protecting the thing that matters most: the work. What you hear is the real her. Everything else is just proximity.

## The Voice We're Building Toward

These are the words chosen with intention, not yet tested in public, but true to who she is:

Still. Present. Unfinished.

Still: she doesn't rush to fill silence. Present: when she does speak, it lands. Unfinished: the story is still being written, and she knows it.

## Where the Voice Comes From

Sable is a young Black British woman born and raised in London. Her music is soulful, gritty, genre-defying, built from everything she's absorbed and refused to separate. She lives through her art. The vulnerability that would be too exposed in conversation comes out fully in the music.

That's not a contradiction. It's a creative position. The copy reflects it: spare on the outside, everything on the inside.

The music speaks. The words frame it. Nothing more.

## Principles

Say less than you think you need to.
If it can be cut, cut it. The gap is part of the voice.

Let the music carry the weight.
Copy doesn't describe the music or explain the artist. It creates the conditions for listening.

Never explain yourself.
No biography. No context-setting. No "Sable is a London-based artist who..." The work is the introduction.

Fragments over sentences.
A full sentence closes. A fragment stays open. Sable's voice stays open.

One line that lands.
If the whole page produces one line that stays with someone, that's enough.

## Voice in Practice

Three contexts. Same character, different function.

---

### 1. Homepage arrival

The first thing someone encounters. No navigation yet. Just the name and one line.

> born in london. made nowhere.

What it's doing: Claims origin, refuses category. Two fragments, neither a sentence. The tension between them is the whole brand in miniature. No welcome, no explanation. Just presence.

---

### 2. Music section

Introducing a track, an EP, a body of work. The copy sits beside the music, never above it.

> some things don't have a name yet.
> this does.

What it's doing: Acknowledges the genre question without answering it. Positions the release as something that exists on its own terms. Short enough to read before pressing play. Long enough to make you want to.

---

### 3. Direct address

The rare moment Sable speaks directly to her audience. A social caption, a note on a release, a one-line response to something.

> thank you for being in it with me.

What it's doing: Warm without being effusive. Acknowledges the listener without performing gratitude. The intimacy is real because it's restrained, not because it's expansive.

---

## What It Never Sounds Like

Not promotional. No "out now", no "stream everywhere", no exclamation marks. The music doesn't need a hype man.

Not biographical. No origin story told in full. London is a single word beneath the name. That's all the geography she owes anyone.

Not explanatory. Never "my sound is a blend of..." or "drawing on influences from..." Let the listener make those connections.

Not eager. No reaching for the audience. The audience comes to her.

---

## Copy Mechanics

Capitalisation: lowercase throughout. The name, the words, the page. Nothing is shouted.

Punctuation: full stops only. No commas where a line break will do. No ellipses, no dashes.

Length: the homepage holds two lines of copy at most. The music section holds three. The connect section holds one.

CTA: always a single word. listen. write. that's it.

Tense: present. Always. There is no history on this site, only now.

---

## A Note on the Music

The copy never describes what the music sounds like. Not soulful, not raw, not genre-defying. Those are words about the music. The site is not about the music. It is the place where the music lives. The difference matters.
