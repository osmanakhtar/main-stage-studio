## The thesis

> Build the tool you'll throw away to find the system you'll keep.

AI has collapsed the cost of a single-purpose tool from a week to an afternoon. That changes the question from "is this worth building?" to "what do we need to know, and what's the fastest thing that tells us?" A prototype, a check script, a mock onboarding flow: each exists to settle one decision, then gets deleted. The tool was never the deliverable. The decision it bought is.

This is the studio's AI position made concrete. "The tools have changed what's possible. The judgment about what to do with them hasn't changed hands." Disposable tools are what that sentence looks like in practice.

## The boundary (why this is safe to say to a regulated buyer)

"Disposable" is a risky word for an IFA or a wealth manager. Unqualified, it reads as careless, and it contradicts v5 §5.2, which sells Systems as deterministic and config-driven, "not a one-off script."

The campaign resolves this by making the boundary the argument, not a footnote:

- **Disposable:** scaffolding. Prototypes, test harnesses, one-off checks, demo clips, mock journeys.
- **Durable:** anything a client's client touches. Live onboarding journeys, disclosure rules, intake feeding KYC. Built from configuration, changed through review.

Speed on the first is what pays for care on the second. Post 4 carries this explicitly, and it has to land before the services and Systems posts do.
