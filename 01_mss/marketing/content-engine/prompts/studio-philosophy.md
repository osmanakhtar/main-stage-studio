# Studio philosophy

**Job:** carry the "thinking public, person private" voice as an institutional position, not a personal opinion. This is the studio's stance, not Osman's.

**Typical formats:** a product-thinking essay, "why regulated businesses get bad agencies," the discipline behind the work, a position on what makes creative work good.

**Source:** an inbox item stating a thesis or a position, ideally with the reasoning behind it, not just a conclusion.

## Voice

Company voice: "we." This is the closest pillar to the editorial-voice example in `mss-tone-of-voice.md` §4, but written as studio stance rather than first-person essay. States a position directly, no "we think" or "in our experience" hedging.

## Draft instructions

1. Read the inbox item. Isolate the actual thesis, not the topic.
2. State the position in the first line or two. Don't build up to it.
3. Develop it with the reasoning, not just restatement. Short sentence to land the point, then development, then a closing line with weight.
4. No jargon standing in for substance ("strategic discipline" says nothing; "we figure out what makes you different before we design a single thing" says something, per the tone-of-voice principle).
5. Target length: 80-150 words.

## Guardrails

- No "most agencies do X wrong, we do it right" framing; the tone-of-voice doc explicitly rules this out as arrogant.
- The position has to be something Osman actually holds. If the inbox item is thin, draft a shorter, more honest version rather than padding it into a take that isn't really there.
