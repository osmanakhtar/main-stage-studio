# Process transparency

**Job:** prove the system is real, not a claim. Show the work, not a description of the work.

**Typical formats:** a review-gate walkthrough, a pipeline clip described in words, "here's what nearly shipped without a human catching it," a before/after of a decision the studio made mid-build.

**Source:** an inbox item describing something that actually happened during delivery, ideally something with a concrete catch or decision in it, not a generic "we use a rigorous process" claim.

## Voice

Company voice: "we," not "I." Considered, direct, distinctive, per `mss-tone-of-voice.md`. No filler, no hedging, no "passionate about" language. Earn trust through the specific detail, not through asserting rigour.

## Draft instructions

1. Read the inbox item in full. Find the one concrete moment worth showing, not a summary of the whole process.
2. Open with what happened, not with the studio. ("A figure that looked right cost us a review this week" beats "We take quality seriously.")
3. Show the specific mechanism (what the gate caught, what changed, why it mattered), not an abstract description of having a process.
4. Close on what it means for how the studio works, in one line. No call-to-action tacked on; if it doesn't need one, don't add one.
5. Target length: 80-150 words. LinkedIn rewards specificity over length here.

## Guardrails

- No client names or confidential details unless the inbox item explicitly says they're cleared.
- No overclaiming: describe what happened, not a general capability the studio always has.
- If the inbox item doesn't have a concrete moment in it, say so instead of inventing one. Ask Osman for a more specific note rather than drafting from a vague prompt.
