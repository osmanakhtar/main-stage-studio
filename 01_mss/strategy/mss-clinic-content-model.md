# MSS Clinic Content Model — Pillars, Capture, and the Run Month

*Created: 24 July 2026*
*Status: PROPOSAL — supports `mss-positioning-v4-vertical.md` (the clinic bet). Nothing here is canon until the founder signs it off and the decisions log is updated.*
*Companion docs: `mss-positioning-v4-vertical.md`, `mss-repositioning-plan.md`, `mss-website-copy-v2.md`.*

**Purpose:** prove that the Clinic Monthly retainer has a sustainable content engine behind it. The open worry from the v4 discussion was "what will a clinic actually push regularly?" This doc answers it: a fixed pillar model fed by a periodic content day, drip-finished by the existing pipeline. If this holds, the retainer holds.

---

## 1. The core mechanic

A clinic is not an event machine. It will not generate or film content daily on its own. So the model does not ask it to.

> **Batch-capture, drip-finish.** MSS runs a half-day content day at the clinic every 4-6 weeks, harvests a bank of raw material, then the pipeline finishes and schedules it across the following weeks. Between content days, the desk-produced pillars (education, offers) carry the calendar with zero clinic effort.

This is the FSC media-day pipeline transferred: capture in a burst, finish and schedule over time. The clinic's only recurring obligation is "be available for a morning every six weeks." Everything else is MSS-side.

Two content sources, and every pillar is tagged to one:
- **Capture-dependent** — needs the clinic, the practitioner, or a patient in front of a camera. Fed by the content day.
- **Desk-produced** — evergreen or calendar-driven, made by MSS without the clinic present.

The health of the model is the ratio: if most of the calendar is desk-produced, the clinic's low effort still yields a full calendar, and a missed content day does not go dark for weeks.

## 2. The pillar model

Six pillars, each doing one job in the funnel. Purpose, format, source, and the compliance flag that shapes it.

| Pillar | Job in the funnel | Typical formats | Source | Compliance load |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Education** | Top-of-funnel trust; capture search intent | Explainer reel, myth-bust, "what to expect", FAQ carousel, aftercare | Desk (evergreen) | Medium — no POM promotion, no misleading claims |
| **2. Results** | Bottom-of-funnel proof; strongest converter | Before/after (consented), result reveal, treatment journey | Content day + ongoing consented captures | High — POM rules, consent, ASA before/after standards |
| **3. Practitioner** | Trust and human connection; the injector is the brand | Face-to-camera authority clip, technique, philosophy, credentials | Content day | Low-medium — claims must be substantiated |
| **4. Experience** | De-risk the nervous first-timer | BTS, the room, first-visit walkthrough, calm/clean signals | Content day (b-roll bank) | Low |
| **5. Patient voice** | Social proof | Filmed testimonial, review graphic, patient story | Content day (filmed) or desk (graphic) | Medium — genuine, not incentivised, consent |
| **6. Offers / seasonal** | The commercial CTA; drives the booking | Package post, seasonal campaign, new-treatment launch, event | Desk (calendar-driven) | Medium — promotional rules, no POM naming |

**Reactive (flex slot):** not a standing pillar. When a relevant story breaks (a botched-work scare, a treatment trend), one scheduled slot is swapped for a timely response. Desk-produced, ad hoc. Held open, not planned.

## 3. The monthly mix

Target cadence: 16 posts / month (~4 per week). Weighted toward desk-produced and top-of-funnel so the calendar survives a slipped content day.

| Pillar | Posts / month | Share | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Education | 5 | 31% | Desk |
| Results | 3 | 19% | Content day |
| Practitioner | 2 | 12% | Content day |
| Experience | 2 | 12% | Content day |
| Patient voice | 2 | 12% | Content day / desk |
| Offers / seasonal | 2 | 12% | Desk |
| **Total** | **16** | **100%** | — |

**Read of the mix:** 9 of 16 posts (~56%) are desk-produced and need no clinic capture at all. Only 7 depend on the content day, and one half-day yields well over 7. The clinic can miss a beat and the calendar does not.

## 4. The content day

**Frequency:** every 4-6 weeks. One session feeds ~6 weeks of capture-dependent pillars.
**Duration:** 3-4 hours on site.
**Present:** MSS (capture + direction), the lead practitioner, any consented patients booked into the window.

**Shot list (target output):**
- 6-8 practitioner talking-head clips — one per queued education/authority topic, batched in one sitting. Each finishes into a reel, some into carousels.
- Treatment b-roll and room/BTS — a bank that covers Experience and dresses other pillars for the next 6 weeks.
- Consented patient results — captured opportunistically from patients in that day's clinic list who have consented.
- 2-3 filmed patient testimonials — if willing patients are in the window.

**Yield target:** enough raw to finish 8-10 capture-dependent posts, i.e. a full cycle plus buffer.

**After the day (pipeline, MSS-side):** ingest → clip and finish → caption in the clinic's voice → compliance lint → Stage review (client approves per post) → schedule. This is the existing Studio pipeline; no new build.

---

## 5. The Run month (rhythm)

Content days land every ~6 weeks, so a given calendar month may or may not contain one. The weekly rhythm is constant regardless.

- **Content-day week (every ~6 weeks):** capture on site; ingest the same day.
- **Every week:** finish the next batch → lint → Stage review → schedule. Client approves everything before it goes anywhere. Site copy change requests handled in the same Stage pass.
- **Desk pillars:** education and offers produced on a rolling basis, independent of the content day, so the calendar is always filled ahead.
- **Reactive slot:** held open; used only when something worth reacting to breaks.

The client experience is unchanged from the v3/v4 Run: one place to review everything, sign-off as the gate, changes that ship.

## 6. Cold-start (a clinic with no material yet)

A new clinic often has no results bank, a camera-shy practitioner, and no filmed testimonials. Do not pretend the results pillar is full on day one.

Ramp:
- **Month 1:** front-load the pillars that need least — Education, Experience, and Practitioner (start with lower-pressure authority topics to warm the practitioner to camera). Offers run from day one.
- **Content days 1-2:** build the results and testimonial banks as consented patients come through. The banks compound.
- **By month 2-3:** the full mix is reachable. Set this expectation in the proposal so the ramp is a plan, not a shortfall.

## 7. Compliance layer (the moat, not a footnote)

UK medical-aesthetics content sits inside real advertising law. This is exactly why the built POM-grade lint is a sellable safety promise, and why the mix leans on education over naive before/afters.

- **POM (prescription-only medicines) cannot be advertised to the public.** Botulinum toxin (Botox) is POM: it cannot be named or promoted in public content. Copy uses "anti-wrinkle" / "wrinkle-relaxing treatment" and stays non-promotional. Dermal fillers are medical devices, not POM, and can be named.
- **Before/after content** must not mislead, requires patient consent, must not trivialise or pressure, and must not target under-18s (ASA/CAP).
- **Claims must be substantiated;** no "safe with no risks", no guaranteed outcomes.
- **The lint enforces this on every post** before it reaches Stage. Most clinics get these rules wrong routinely; a pipeline that checks each post is a genuine, ownable promise. It is not legal cover — see v4 §8 risk 3; the contract needs a scope-of-responsibility clause before compliance is sold as a safety claim.

## 8. Why this answers the worry

The concern was content supply. The model resolves it on three fronts:
1. **Topics are not scarce.** Education alone is near-infinite; most pillars are commercially motivated (they drive consults), not vanity.
2. **Capture is the real constraint, and it is solved by batching.** The clinic gives one morning every six weeks; the pipeline does the rest. This is proven IP (the FSC media day), not a hope.
3. **The calendar is resilient.** ~56% desk-produced means a slipped content day does not cause a dark week.

Honest limit: clinic content wins on consistency and conversion, not virality. It will not ride built-in drama the way a fight callout does. That makes it more repeatable (easier to systematise) and less exciting to make. Name that trade-off; do not sell past it.
