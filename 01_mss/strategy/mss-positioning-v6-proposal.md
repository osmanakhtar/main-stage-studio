## 1. The short version

MSS has spent five months describing itself in the abstract ("presence partner", "a better system", "Presence and Systems") while quietly building a set of very concrete things that work. The work is ahead of the brand. This proposal turns the work into the brand.

**Six products, each one already demonstrated** (a seventh, Review, added 11 Oct 2026, §5.9): Identity, Website, Campaign, Content, Platform, and Care (the monthly running cost).

**One idea that ties them together:** *Run the show.* Everything MSS builds is handed over with the controls. The client edits their own site, writes and approves their own emails, owns their own customer records and code. That is what PureMed actually has today, and it is the thing almost nobody else at this price point offers.

**A new identity to match:** off the quiet parchment-and-serif boutique look, onto a stage: warm black, house-light white, one tungsten spotlight colour, a wide confident grotesque, a mono face for the system layer, and a mark built from stage spike tape.

**A sharper, more honest voice:** plain, specific, a little swagger. Prices on the page. Receipts, not adjectives.

## 2. Why change: what the current brand gets wrong

### 2.1 The positioning has moved five times and the proof has never been where the pitch is

| Version | Date | Who it was for | Proof behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 to v2 | June | Founders with a vision | Self-initiated pieces |
| v3 | 12 Jul | Presence partner for established small businesses | Combat sports content operation |
| v4 | 23 Jul | Aesthetics clinics | A fictional demo clinic (Vera) |
| v5 | 4 Aug | Regulated professional services | The founder's CV (which cannot be shown) and fictional booking-engine tenants |

Every version picked an audience first and then went looking for evidence. v5 is the most honest of them, but it points the whole studio at accountants, brokers and IFAs, a market where MSS has **no client, no live work and no case study**, while the real, live, paying work sits in a clinic, an artist and a fight promotion.

What changed since v5 locked: PureMed went live on 30 September 2026. Not a demo. A 20-page site the owner edits herself, a customer platform with consent records and email campaigns she writes and approves, a lead-magnet funnel, a migration from her old booking and records systems, and a signed DPIA and DPA. v4 was dropped because clinics had "zero real client proof". That reason no longer holds.

**The fix is to stop positioning by audience and start positioning by product.** Products are stable. They do not need to be re-argued every time a new kind of client turns up. The audience then becomes "whoever needs these things", filtered by a values test (§4), not by sector.

### 2.2 The offer is invisible

"Presence" and "Systems" are categories, not things. "Launch / Presence Monthly / Campaign" are stages of a relationship, not products. A prospect landing on the site today cannot answer three basic questions: *What can I buy? What does it cost? What do I get at the end?* Systems is explicitly "not packaged, a conversation, not a price list", which for a cold buyer reads as "expensive and vague".

### 2.3 The hero line fails the specificity test

"You don't need a bigger team. You need a better system." Swap in any automation agency, any SaaS tool, any fractional-COO consultancy, and the line still works. When a competitor can say your line, it is not your line. It also sells the mechanism (the system) rather than the outcome the client feels.

### 2.4 The visual identity is the category default

Parchment, terracotta, ember, blush; Cormorant Garamond over a geometric sans; editorial stillness. This is the dominant look of independent creative studios from roughly 2022 to 2025. It says "tasteful boutique". It does not say "this person can migrate 1,600 appointment records and get a DPIA signed". The brand currently undersells the hardest, rarest thing the studio does.

Specific problems:

- **It is quiet where the founder is not.** The founder vision is rooted in hip hop, non-conformity and the right to be heard. The current identity is soft, warm and polite. The voice doc even says the non-conformist spine "never appears on the surface". That is the wrong call: it is the most distinctive thing about the studio and it is buried.
- **Cormorant is fragile.** Light-weight, high-contrast serifs break down at small sizes, on low-quality screens, and inside product UI. MSS now ships product UI (the console, Stage). The brand has to work there too.
- **The mark needs a paragraph to explain.** Two offset squares whose colour meanings had to be formally corrected on 6 August because they had been read backwards. A mark that needs a correction notice is not doing its job.
- **It collides with client work.** Warm neutrals and serif display are also where aesthetics-clinic and artist brands live. MSS's own identity should sit clearly apart from the clients it presents.

### 2.5 The voice is beautiful and too long

"Some things are hard to say not because they're unclear, but because no one has ever given them the right form." Lovely sentence. A clinic owner on a phone between patients does not finish it. The current voice leads with empathy and arrives at capability late. Buyers at this size buy the capability, and trust the empathy once they see it in how they are treated.

### 2.6 The founder is hidden

"The thinking is public. The person stays private." For a studio of one, selling trust, this costs more than it protects. Every buyer is buying Osman. The site should say so (§8.4 sets out what stays private).

## 3. The insight

> **Owner-led businesses want to look as good as the work they do, but every upgrade leaves them more dependent on someone else (a designer to change a word, a developer to change a form, four logins to send one email), because studios sell the launch and keep the keys.**

Evidence it is true, from MSS's own work:

- PureMed had up to four separate stores for one customer (old site, Mailchimp, Faces, Acuity). Changing a price meant asking someone.
- Ayesha's whole brief comes down to "be more visible and sell", without becoming dependent on a platform she does not control.
- The FSC content role existed because the promotion could not keep a weekly pace without someone outside running it.

The tension MSS resolves: **looking professional usually means losing control. MSS makes it mean gaining control.**

## 4. Positioning v6

### 4.1 Positioning statement

> For owner-led businesses whose name is on the door, Main Stage Studio builds the brand, the website, the content and the systems behind them, then hands over the controls. Unlike agencies that sell the launch and rent you the result, everything MSS builds is yours to run: you edit it, you approve it, you own it.

### 4.2 Who it is for

**Owner-led, reputation-led businesses, roughly 1 to 20 people, where the owner is the brand.** Practitioners, clinics, consultants, independent professional firms, artists and makers, promoters and event businesses.

This deliberately absorbs v5 rather than throwing it away: regulated professional firms are inside this set, and for them the compliance literacy (consent, DPIA, claims checking) is a genuine edge. They just stop being the only door in.

**The values filter stays** (it is good and it is true): "drawn to people whose work means something beyond the commercial." It moves from closing line to selection criterion.

**Who it is not for:** businesses that want an agency team, enterprise procurement, or anyone who wants to be locked in and managed.

### 4.3 What makes it different (three things, all provable)

1. **You run it.** Live editing on your own site, emails you write and approve, a console you log into. Not a promise: it is how PureMed works today.
2. **You own it.** Static site code in your repo, customer records in a database under your control, no proprietary builder, no platform hostage. Leave any time and take everything.
3. **One studio, the whole show.** Brand, site, content, and the operational system underneath, built to fit each other, by one person who has spent two decades building systems that were not allowed to fail.

### 4.4 What carries over from v5

- The founder credential, now as the reason the Platform product is trustworthy, not as the headline.
- The sign-off gate: nothing goes live without the client's approval. Reframed as "your cue" (§6).
- The FSC anonymisation rule and the employer anonymisation rule. Both unchanged.
- The Presence Monthly founding band, now the Content product price.

## 6. The brand idea

### 6.1 The platform

> **Run the show.**

Three words that work three ways:

1. **For the client:** you are in charge of your own business's front of house. Not the agency, not the platform.
2. **For the name:** Main Stage is the headline slot. MSS gets the client onto it and hands them the mic.
3. **For the founder:** in hip hop, the artists who won were the ones who owned their masters. Internally, that is the whole brand in one sentence: *MSS builds things the client owns.* The phrase "own your masters" stays an internal north star and a journal piece; it does not go in the hero, because a clinic owner will not decode it.

### 6.2 The vocabulary system

A light stage vocabulary, used where it clarifies and dropped where it would be twee:

| Plain step | Brand name | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery call | Soundcheck | How it works, proposals |
| Prototype review on Stage | Rehearsal | How it works, client emails |
| Client sign-off | Your cue | Everywhere. "Nothing goes live until you give the cue." |
| Launch | Showtime | How it works |
| Monthly running | The run | Content and Care |

Rule: product names stay plain (Identity, Website, Campaign, Content, Platform, Care). The stage vocabulary is for the process, never for what someone is buying.

### 6.3 Lines

- **Hero:** Run the show.
- **Support line:** Brand, website, content and the systems behind them. Built by one studio. Handed over with the controls.
- **Proof line:** Everything we build, you can change, approve and take with you.
- **Gate line:** Nothing goes live until you give the cue.
- **Closing line:** If what you're building matters to you, it matters here. *(kept from v5)*

## 7. Visual identity

### 7.1 Concept: the lit stage

A stage is dark so the thing on it can be seen. MSS's identity is mostly black and white, with one warm spotlight colour that only ever lands on the thing that matters: a call to action, a price, a client's work, the "live" moment. The studio is the stage and the lighting rig. The client is what is lit.

This keeps the one genuinely good idea in the current mark (MSS is the structure, the client is what it makes visible) and makes it obvious without a caption.

### 7.2 Colour

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Stage Black | `#12110F` | Default background on the site, all type on light surfaces |
| Riser | `#1F1D1A` | Raised surfaces on dark (panels, cards, code) |
| House Lights | `#F3F1EC` | Light surfaces: case studies, documents, proposals, long reading |
| Rigging Grey | `#8C8981` | Secondary text, rules, captions |
| Tungsten | `#FFB21A` | The spotlight. CTAs, prices, the mark, one key moment per screen |
| Cue Red | `#E5402A` | Signals only: "live", recording, errors. Never decorative |

Rules:

- One Tungsten moment per view. If two things are lit, nothing is.
- Tungsten is never text on House Lights (contrast fails). On light surfaces it is a fill with Stage Black text on it.
- The site is dark-first. Documents and long-read case studies sit on House Lights.
- Client case studies keep the client's own palette (existing rule, kept). Tungsten frames them; it never recolours them.
- Still banned: purple and blue AI gradients, glassmorphism, gradient backgrounds.

### 7.3 Type

| Role | Typeface | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Archivo**, expanded width (wdth 112 to 125), weight 700 to 800 | Wide, flat, confident. Reads like billing on a poster, holds up at any size, has the width axis so one family does both jobs. Google Fonts |
| Body and UI | **Archivo**, normal width, 400 to 500 | Same family, so product UI and marketing feel like one thing |
| System layer | **Geist Mono**, 400 to 500 | Labels, prices, timelines, "receipts", the cue sheet. Signals the engineering side honestly. Google Fonts |

Retired: Cormorant Garamond, Plus Jakarta Sans. Avoided on purpose: Anton (FSC's face), Inter (generic), anything used in the Ayesha directions (Syne, Bricolage Grotesque, Gloock, Instrument Serif) so client and studio stay distinct.

Typographic rules: headlines tight, left-aligned, set large; all-caps only in Geist Mono labels; body max 65ch; prices and timelines always in mono.

### 7.4 The mark: Spike

Stage managers mark the exact spot where a performer stands with two strips of tape at the corners: a spike mark. MSS's new mark is exactly that: two Tungsten corners, top-left and bottom-right, framing empty space.

It reads three ways without explanation: a **spike mark** (your spot on the stage), a **crop frame** (we frame your work), and a **selection box** (you can edit what's inside). The third one is the product.

### 7.5 Imagery

- **The real work, shown as it is used:** screenshots and screen recordings of the client's site being edited, the console, the approval moment, a phone receiving the email. This is the strongest visual asset MSS has and it currently shows none of it.
- **Hard-lit still life on black:** objects from each client's world under one spotlight (a clinic's instruments, an artist's brushes, a fight glove). Generated with Higgsfield, single hard light, deep shadow, no people unless approved.
- **Client work at full bleed in its own colours,** framed by the spike corners.
- **No stock people, no glossy AI portraits, no laptops-on-desks.** Where AI imagery is used, it is a craft demonstration and the case study says so.

### 7.6 Motion

Kept from the current rules and sharpened: motion is a lighting cue, not decoration. The one signature move is the spotlight: Tungsten fades up on the key element as it enters view. `transform` and `opacity` only.

### 7.8 Alternative direction: Glass Box (added 10 Oct 2026)

Three specimens now sit side by side on Stage (engagement `mss-v6-brand`): **A Stage** (dark, Tungsten), **B Curtain** (paper, theatre red), **C Glass Box** (liquid glass, day and night). Files: `01_mss/brand/mss-v6-specimen{,-b,-c}.html`.

**The brief behind C:** product pages should open on short videos of the tooling, with a liquid-glass look.

**Verdict.** As a pure aesthetic, liquid glass fails. It is the most saturated look of 2025 to 2026 (Apple's iOS 26 material, most of 21st.dev), and it reads as AI startup or SaaS, which is wrong for an owner-led buyer. It holds when glass has one job: **glass is only ever the control layer** (nav, edit dock, approve button, toggles, captions) over real moving work. That is MSS's product, a clear layer of controls on top of the client's business. *A glass box, not a black box.*

- **The videos and the glass are one idea.** Glass is invisible over a flat background and only works with rich content behind it. The tooling videos are that content.
- **Positioning unchanged; persona shifts.** The products, ICP, prices and "Run the show" all stay. The persona moves from the performer (A) to the clear operator: calm, precise, nothing hidden. The new proof line is "See everything. Change anything. Own all of it."
- **Score:** about 7.9 on its own, about 8.3 combined with "Run the show" (A alone was 8.1). Not a 9: the material is borrowed. What makes it ownable is the rule (controls only) and what sits behind the glass.
- **Costs:** less of the founder's edge (A carries that best), the performance of blur over video on phones, and contrast over moving content.
- **Day and night:** one accent across both (Tungsten, carried from A). Night is the default for the tools. In day, accent words become ink with a Tungsten highlighter band, because amber text on light reads muddy.

**Glass rules (found while building C):**
1. Glass only on controls, never on content cards or reading text.
2. Something real is always behind it.
3. Glass carries its own scrim (72% night, 70% day) sized for the worst frame underneath. Only full-strength type goes on glass: muted grey on glass measured 2.8:1 over bright content and fails.
4. Reduced transparency and browsers without blur get solid panels (checked). Reduced motion freezes every loop on its final frame.
5. Demos use fictional data only (Vera, MSS). Never real client data.

**Video fit across A, B and C:** the video idea works in all three. A's dark stage flatters glowing screens. B's paper is the weakest backdrop for video. C is built around the videos. The mock-ups in C are placeholders; real recordings (Playwright against the local `vera` tenant and a Stage demo engagement) come after a direction is chosen.

## 8. Tone of voice

### 8.1 Character

**Plain. Specific. A bit of swagger.**

From "quietly confident and genuinely empathetic" to "confident, direct, and on your side". The empathy stays. It shows in what MSS does (hands over control, publishes prices, writes in plain English), not in long sentences about feelings.

### 8.2 Principles

1. **Name the thing.** Say what they get, in nouns. "A 10-page site you can edit yourself", not "a digital presence".
2. **Show the receipt.** Dates, page counts, what was built, what it replaced. A number beats an adjective. Every number passes the figures gate.
3. **Talk like the owner.** First person, "I", because it is one person. "We" only when it genuinely means the studio and the client together.
4. **Hand over the mic.** Clients speak in their own words on case studies. MSS does not narrate other people's feelings.
5. **Short wins.** If a line works at half the length, use half.
6. **Point of view, out loud.** The non-conformist spine is allowed on the surface now. MSS has opinions: about agencies keeping the keys, about rented websites, about AI slop. It says them.

### 8.3 Before and after

| Context | Current (v5) | Proposed (v6) |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | You don't need a bigger team. You need a better system. | Run the show. |
| Offer | Presence, and the systems underneath it. | Six things I build. Prices on the page. |
| Website product | A website built as a fast, static site. Nothing to update, nothing to break, nothing you'll be invoiced for maintaining. | A fast site you change yourself. Typo on a Sunday night? Fix it. No ticket, no invoice. |
| Gate | Sign-off isn't a courtesy. It's the gate the system enforces. | Nothing goes live until you give the cue. |
| Credential | Main Stage Studio runs on a proven track record, not a portfolio built to look the part. It was built by someone who spent years inside the parts of business that aren't allowed to get it wrong. | I spent twenty years building systems that weren't allowed to fail: TV channels, banks, onboarding for millions of people. Your website is not the hard part of my week. |
| AI | The tools have changed what's possible... | AI does the typing. I do the deciding. You do the approving. |

### 8.4 What still stays private

The founder becomes visible as a professional: name, face, career in outline, point of view. Still private: employers by name, family, faith, anything from the founder vision document. The vision informs the voice; it is not published.

### 8.5 Never sounds like

Agency-speak ("solutions", "end-to-end", "elevate"), false scale ("our team"), hedging, fake urgency, and anything that would embarrass a regulated client if they forwarded it to their compliance person.

## Appendix A: Creative director working

**Brief level:** business-level and brand-level idea (repositioning the company and its platform), plus a tagline. Not a campaign.

**Insight (Pollard format):** Owner-led businesses want to look as good as the work they do, but every upgrade leaves them more dependent on someone else, because studios sell the launch and keep the keys.

**Tensions found:**
- Category: studios promise "your brand", then deliver something the client cannot change without them.
- Human: owners want to look established without losing control of their own business.
- Founder: a non-conformist founder running a polite, quiet brand.

**Methods used:** structural (SIT subtraction: remove the agency from the after-launch picture), association (bisociation: music industry ownership x small business websites), inversion (reverse brainstorm: "how would we make the client as dependent as possible?", then flip each answer).

**Routes generated** (first three are warmups):

| # | Route | One line | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Better system (current) | You don't need a bigger team | Fails specificity test |
| 2 | Careful, in public | Regulated-grade studio | Narrow, no proof in sector |
| 3 | AI-powered studio | Agency output at studio cost | Saturated, and the founder rightly hates "AI slop" positioning |
| 4 | **Run the show** | Everything handed over with the controls | **Recommended** |
| 5 | The Lineup | Festival-poster identity, products as acts on the bill | Strong visually, too gimmicky for a clinic or solicitor buyer. Its poster typography survives inside route 4 |
| 6 | Open Kitchen | Radical transparency: public prices, public build logs | Strong behaviour, weak as a whole brand. Survives as the published-prices decision and "show the receipt" |
| 7 | Own your masters | Music-industry ownership as the brand | Founder-true and original, but the buyer cannot decode it. Kept as the internal north star and a journal piece |
| 8 | Built to leave | "I build it so you never need me" | Uncomfortable in a good way, but undercuts Care and Content revenue. Its honesty survives in "take everything with you" |
| 9 | Headline act | "Your business, top of the bill" | Flattering, generic |
| 10 | One person, whole stage | Studio of one, stated proudly | True, but about MSS, not the client |

**Scores for the top three** (weighted: originality 0.25, strategic fit 0.20, emotion 0.20, feasibility 0.15, scalability 0.10, simplicity 0.10):

| Route | Orig | Fit | Emotion | Feas | Scale | Simple | Weighted | HumanKind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Run the show | 7 | 9 | 7 | 9 | 9 | 9 | **8.1** | 7 |
| Open Kitchen | 7 | 7 | 6 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 7.1 | 6 |
| The Lineup | 8 | 5 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 6.9 | 6 |

**Why "Run the show" is not a 9:** the phrase is common English, so originality is capped at 7; it only becomes ownable through the behaviour behind it (live editing, owner-written emails, owned code), which competitors cannot copy quickly. The emotion is Tier 2 (pride, autonomy) rather than Tier 3. It earns its place on strategic fit and on being true today, not on cleverness. That is the right trade for a studio of one selling trust.

**Kill-your-darlings check:** the strongest argument against it is that "run the show" could read as "you'll be doing all the work". The support line and the Care and Content products answer that directly: you *can* run it, and I'll run it for you if you'd rather. Argument noted; it does not outweigh the idea.

**Pre-mortem (it is a year later and v6 failed, why?):** prices stayed at founding level too long; Platform was sold before the liability clause and one incident damaged trust; the founder's day job left no time for Content clients; the stage vocabulary got overused and turned twee. Each has a guard in §5.8, §11 item 9, §5.4 (three-month minimum, capacity cap to be set) and §6.2.
