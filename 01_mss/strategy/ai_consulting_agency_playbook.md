# AI Consulting Agency Operational Playbook
### From $0 to $25,000/Month — A Step-by-Step Operating Guide

> **Source:** Video transcript by the founder of Leftclick & Maker School  
> **Framework:** The Five Pillars — Marketing → Sales → Onboarding → Fulfillment → Retention

---

## Overview

This playbook covers the full operational lifecycle of an AI consulting agency, structured in the exact order a lead flows through your business. Each section includes tactical scripts, trade-off analysis, and references to accompanying templates and resources.

The five pillars are:

1. **Marketing** — Generate leads
2. **Sales** — Convert leads to clients
3. **Onboarding** — Set the relationship up for success
4. **Fulfillment** — Deliver and manage perception
5. **Retention** — Turn one-off projects into long-term revenue

---

## Pillar 1: Marketing

> **Core principle:** If you are at $0 revenue and spending time on anything other than directly generating leads, you are wasting money.

Marketing is the foundation of the entire business. Without a consistent flow of leads, everything else is irrelevant. The four mechanisms below are the only ones that reliably work for beginners today.

### Marketing Method Comparison

| Method | Time Investment | Capital Required | Barrier to Entry | Scalability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Platform Sales (Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com) | High | Low | Low | Low |
| Cold Email | Low (post-setup) | Medium (upfront) | Very High | Very High |
| Communities (Slack, Skool, Reddit) | Very High | Very Low | Low | Low–Medium |
| Cold DMs (LinkedIn, Instagram, X, Facebook) | High | Low | Very High | Medium |

---

### 1.1 Platform Sales (Upwork / Fiverr / Freelancer.com)

**Why start here:** Platforms put you directly in front of buyers who already know their problem and are ready to pay. You skip the attention and convincing stages entirely.

**Key requirements on Upwork:**
- A standout CV/profile

### 1.2 Cold Email

Cold email is the highest-leverage marketing channel once mastered — it scales independently of your time (20 emails/day or 2,000 emails/day, same system).

**Cold email structure (via Instantly framework):**

1. Personalisation
2. Value proposition
3. Niche reference
4. Their goal
5. Relevant case study
6. Cliffhanger
7. CTA (Call to Action)

**Example script:**
> *"Hey Sarah, I've been seeing XYZ Wellness everywhere recently — huge work. I help health companies automate their onboarding and reduce churn with a $15,000/month minimum guarantee. Just helped a supplement company add 32K in revenue in one month. Can I show you how I do the same for you? No strings."*

## Pillar 2: Sales

> **Core principle:** Sales is not about tricking people. It is about uncovering their pain, showing how your solution solves that pain, and positioning yourself as the obvious investment.

The sales call runs 30–45 minutes and follows six structured steps.

### Sales Call Structure

| Step | Action | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build Rapport | ~1 min |
| 2 | Set the Schedule | ~2 min |
| 3 | Discovery | ~15 min |
| 4 | Propose Solutions | ~10 min |
| 5 | Frame the Investment | ~5 min |
| 6 | Send Proposal | Post-call |

---

### 2.1 Rapport

Get them to smile. A light compliment, a joke, or a simple location question works.

> *"Hey, where are you based? No way — I love that city. How long have you been there?"*

Transition out with: *"Alright, ready to jump in?"*

---

### 2.2 Set the Schedule

Take control of the call by laying out a roadmap upfront.

> *"Here's how these calls usually go — first, I want to understand why you booked. Then I'll dig a bit deeper into your business. Finally, if I think we can help, I'll show you what that looks like and we'll talk next steps. Does that sound good?"*

---

### 2.3 Discovery

This is the most important stage. Ask the right questions and the client will give you everything you need to close.

**High-ROI discovery questions:**

- *"Why did you decide to book this call?"* — Highest ROI question; almost nobody asks it.
- *"What is your goal right now?"* — Establishes a north star to frame your solution against.
- *"Assuming we fix this, what do you think it will do for the company?"* — Gets them to quantify the outcome.
- *"Why haven't you done this yet?"* — Surfaces hidden blockers and urgency.

**Rule:** Ask relevant follow-ups. If their goal is to close more clients, ask: *"How much does a client pay when they come in the door?"*

---

### 2.4 Propose Solutions

Once you have the full picture, position your system as the logical solution.

> *"We've solved many similar problems to X before — this is a great fit for our system. To make a long story short, we're going to solve X by doing Y. My hope is it'll save you Z per month. Would you like to see an example of how this works?"*

**Operational tip:** Maintain a demo library of 5–10 systems you routinely sell. If a client says yes, you duplicate the template and spend 15 minutes customising it. This is productised, templated sales.

---

### 2.5 Frame the Investment

**Always drop the price last.** By this point, value has been built up through discovery and demonstration.

> *"Based on what you've told me — to save you that $50,000 cost — our project would be $19,000. Assuming nothing else needs to be added on, your investment pays for itself in the first month."*

**Critical rule:** After you say the price — **stop talking.** The first person to speak loses.

**Handling objections:**
- **"Can you do it cheaper?"** → Remove line items or features from scope. Do not simply discount.
- **"I need to think about it."** → This is almost never the real reason. Ask: *"Would this system solve the needs you've put in front of me?"* Their answer reveals the true objection.
- **"I need to talk to my spouse/partner."** → That is fine. Send a detailed proposal and let it follow up automatically. Don't pressure.

---

### 2.6 Send the Proposal

Because you're delivering technical solutions, a written proposal with a defined scope is non-negotiable. It prevents scope creep and expectation mismatches.

## Pillar 3: Onboarding

> **Core principle:** Onboarding sets the tone for the entire relationship. Front-load everything into a single kickoff call.

A poor onboarding experience kills upsell probability and creates ongoing operational chaos. A great kickoff call takes ~30 minutes and covers five steps.

### Kickoff Call Structure

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introductions & Framing |
| 2 | Goals & Timelines |
| 3 | Communication Expectations |
| 4 | Platform Access & 2FA |
| 5 | Wrap-Up |

---

### 3.1 Introductions & Framing

Thank them for joining. Introduce everyone on the call. Set the frame immediately.

> *"My goal by the end of this call is for our company to have everything we need — so we don't have to bother you for permissions every day. I want to cover timelines, how we'll communicate, and walk through any platform access we need. Does that sound good?"*

This signals competence and distinguishes you from freelancers who drag logistics out for weeks.

---

### 3.2 Goals & Timelines

Lay out the project roadmap clearly.

> *"Our goal is to start today and wrap up within 3 weeks. The project will be split into three clear milestones so you always know where things stand."*

Clients who have been burned by disappearing consultants will immediately recognise and appreciate this structure.

---

### 3.3 Communication Expectations

Set firm boundaries immediately. This eliminates 90% of ongoing back-and-forth chaos.

> *"You'll get one daily update Monday to Friday over [Slack / Email / WhatsApp]. It'll include where the project's at, what's coming next, and any blockers I need help with. One-off questions will be batched and covered in that daily check-in. Does that work?"*

**Rule:** Any question that comes in between updates gets held for the next check-in — not answered immediately.

---

### 3.4 Platform Access & 2FA

Do not send a list of logins and wait. Handle it live on the call.

> *"Today we need admin access to your Slack and your [platform]. Let's set that up right now — if a two-factor authentication code pops up, just read it to me and we'll proceed. We can get this done in 3 minutes."*

**Why this matters:** Handling access live means the client is signed in to their own workspace. When you deliver the project, there is no handoff — no resetting up, no duplication of work. Everything is already in place.

---

### 3.5 Wrap-Up

> *"Great — any questions? Perfect. We're off to the races. I'll give you your first update tomorrow."*

## Pillar 4: Fulfillment

> **Core principle:** Fulfillment is not about delivering the system. It is about managing the perception of the engagement.

If a client cannot see or feel progress, they will assume none is happening. Split fulfillment into two operational tracks: **Communication** and **Delivery**.

---

### 4.1 Communication

Send a daily check-in Monday–Friday. It takes 60 seconds per client and dramatically increases trust, retention, and upsell rates.

**Daily check-in template:**

> *"Hey [Client], just checking in — no action needed from your end. We just finished [X]. We just started [Y]. I'll let you know how things are going tomorrow. Thanks."*

**Rules:**
- Keep it short and bulleted
- Clearly flag any action items the client needs to take
- Use a consistent messaging platform (Slack recommended)

> 📎 **Resource — Daily Communication Templates (Multiple Variations)**  
> Copy-paste daily update templates for client communications are available via the **description link** in the source video. Can also be fed into an AI agent to automate delivery.

**Key insight:** The thing that differentiates top agencies is not the quality of the service — it is the ability to manage the perception of that service better than anyone else. Consistent daily communication alone can justify doubling your prices.

---

### 4.2 Delivery

Package deliverables like products, not raw outputs. Treat the system like a diamond — it needs a box, wrapping, and a bow.

**Delivery checklist:**
- [ ] Core system or automation delivered
- [ ] 10–15 minute Loom video walkthrough recorded
- [ ] Written documentation attached
- [ ] Delivery email sent using a polished template

## Pillar 5: Retention

> **Core principle:** The best time to sell a follow-up project is immediately after you have delivered exceptional work — not weeks later.

Retention converts single projects into ongoing revenue. Most consultants skip this entirely, leaving significant money on the table.

### Retention Operating Procedure

**Step 1: Overdeliver by 5%**

Do not just deliver what was promised. Add one small extra that was noticed during the project.

> *"Hey, here's your Slack workflow — and while I was building it, I noticed [X issue], so I went ahead and fixed that too. I've also attached some documentation."*

This triggers reciprocity and substantially increases the probability of a yes on follow-up work.

**Step 2: Pitch the Follow-Up Project**

Use the reciprocity window immediately after delivery.

> *"While setting up your Slack system, I noticed your funnel wasn't sending SMS messages. I work with a bunch of 7–8 figure businesses and they all use SMS. I think fixing this could add $10–15K/month to your bottom line. I know it's a bold ask — but would you be open to me showing you how this works, whether or not you work with me?"*

**Step 3: Offer a Managed Service**

If the client is happy, pitch an ongoing retainer for funnel optimisation, system maintenance, or continued automation work.
