### 2. Hero
*Register: First impression. Empathy before capability. Unchanged from v3/v4 per v5 §4, hero line and body still hold.*

**Headline:**

> You don't need a bigger team.
> You need a better system.

**Body:**

> Most businesses know exactly what a strong presence looks like. A website that reflects the quality of the work. Content that stays alive past the first month. Words that actually sound like you, and that hold up to scrutiny as well as they read.
>
> What they don't have is the team to keep all of that running, and hiring one costs more than the problem does.
>
> Main Stage Studio builds your presence, then runs it. Brand, website, content: made in one system, reviewed by you in one place, kept moving week after week.

**CTAs:** `See how it works` · `Start a project`

---

### 3. The gap
*Register: Editorial. A position stated, not opinion hedged. Tuned for a regulated buyer per v5 §3 (was general SMB pain in v3/v4).*

**Heading:**

> Launched is not the same as alive.

**Body:**

> Every regulated business has lived some version of this. The site went live and hasn't caught up with what's changed since. The content had a strong first month, then whoever was doing it got pulled back onto client work. The copy was written by someone who never had to think about what they're actually allowed to say, and it shows to the one person in the room who does.
>
> None of that is a motivation problem. Keeping a presence alive, in a way that survives scrutiny as well as it survives a scroll, is a production job that sits awkwardly between what a small regulated firm can staff and what it's safe to outsource. Generalist agencies don't know what you can't say. Freelancers cover one skill of the five it needs.
>
> That gap is what this studio was built to close.

### 5. What we do
*Register: Discovery framing. The logic of the offer, not a service menu. Restructured for v5's two-line architecture (§5): Presence stays a packaged trio, Systems is new and deliberately not packaged.*

**Heading:**

> Presence, and the systems underneath it.

**Intro line:**

> Most engagements are about how you show up. Some are about what's actually running underneath. This studio does both, and doesn't pretend they're the same job.

**Presence**

> **Launch.** We start with what you're actually trying to say, the brief is rarely the real brief, and finding the real one is the work. From there: positioning, a voice that's recognisably yours, a visual identity, and a website built as a fast, static site. Nothing to update, nothing to break, nothing you'll be invoiced for maintaining. You review every stage in one place, and nothing moves forward without your sign-off.
>
> **Presence Monthly.** A monthly engagement that keeps your presence moving, built for how your business is actually regulated, not adapted from a template built for someone else's. A planned calendar, designed posts, edited clips, captions in your voice, every piece checked against your specific rules before you ever see it, and every piece approved by you before it goes anywhere. Your website stays alive the same way: need the wording changed, a price updated, a service added? Ask for it, approve it, and it's live, no ticket queue, no small-change invoice.
>
> **Campaign.** Building toward a launch, a new service, or a renewal season? A time-boxed campaign gets built around it, and it's often where a Presence Monthly engagement actually starts.

**Systems**

> Some of what a regulated business needs isn't public. It's the onboarding flow, the intake process, the decision logic that has to be right every single time, not just look right. That work is scoped and quoted on its own terms, not packaged, because bespoke and fixed-price don't mix honestly. If that's what you need, the first step is a conversation, not a price list.

### 6. The machine, working
*Register: Discovery framing → one editorial beat at the end. Nav label stays "How it works" for wayfinding. Reordered below "Who's behind it" per v5 §4 (demonstration now supports the credential, isn't the centre of gravity). Carries v3's system section forward with one added line naming the secondary execution proof, anonymised per the standing FSC rule.*

**Heading:**

> One place to see everything. One gate everything passes through.

**Body:**

> Behind the studio is a production system built for the studio's own use, because the Presence Monthly side of this work is only affordable if the mechanical parts are automated and the judgment parts never are.
>
> What you see of it is simple. Every piece of work, pages, posts, clips, copy changes, lands in one private review space, on one link, behind your login. You approve it, ask for a change, or flag it for a conversation. From your desk or your phone.
>
> Sign-off isn't a courtesy. It's the gate the system enforces. Nothing ships without it, and everything that ships is exactly what you approved.
>
> It's the same system that runs weekly content for a live combat sports promotion, one person, one system, event pace, every week, without missing a date. If it holds there, it holds for something smaller.

**The honesty paragraph (AI, locked framing):**

> The tools have changed what's possible. A studio of one can now plan, produce, and publish at a pace that used to take a team. But the judgment about what to make, what it should say, and whether it's good enough hasn't changed hands, and here, it never will. A person picks the moment. A person signs the words. The system just makes sure the rest keeps up.

## Quality check (copywriting skill) — run 5 August 2026

- First sentences start on the thing: check (hero opens on the reader's knowledge; gap opens on a lived scenario; Who's behind it opens on the claim itself, not an approach to it)
- Empathy before capability: check (hero and gap precede any offer or credential)
- Active voice, no hedges: check
- Ending closes with weight: check (Start section closes on "it matters here"; The promise closes on "that stays human, on purpose")
- No buzzwords: "end-to-end," "holistic," "solutions" absent; "system" used deliberately and defined by what the client sees, now doing double duty across Presence and Systems
- Honesty constraints from v3/v4/v5: check (no auto-publish claim, no deploy track-record claim, no client counts, no employer name or employer work shown, PureMed not shown while parked)
- Repetition watch: "system" carries real weight across hero, Who's behind it, What we do, and The machine, deliberate as the spine of the page given the hero line, but reads close to the line on overuse, worth a read-aloud pass at prototype stage
- Read aloud: hero, gap, and Who's behind it scan clean; §6 heading duo (on-page vs nav label) is a deliberate split, confirm it doesn't read as a mismatch once built
