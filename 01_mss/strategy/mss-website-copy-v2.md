### 2. Hero
*Register: First impression. Empathy before capability.*

**Headline:**

> You don't need a bigger team.
> You need a better system.

**Body:**

> Most businesses know exactly what a strong presence looks like. A website that reflects the quality of the work. Social accounts that stay alive past the first month. Words that actually sound like you.
>
> What they don't have is the team to keep all of that running — and hiring one costs more than the problem.
>
> Main Stage Studio builds your presence, then runs it. Brand, website, content: made in one system, reviewed by you in one place, kept moving week after week.

**CTAs:** `See how it works` · `Start a project`

---

### 3. The gap
*Register: Editorial. A position stated, not opinion hedged.*

**Heading:**

> Launched is not the same as alive.

**Body:**

> Every business has lived some version of this. The website went live and hasn't changed since. The Instagram had a strong first month. The copy was written by someone who was never in the room.
>
> None of that is a motivation problem. Keeping a presence alive is a production job — planning, writing, designing, editing, publishing, every week — and it sits awkwardly between what you can staff and what you can sensibly outsource. Agencies charge team rates for it. Freelancers cover one skill of the five it needs.
>
> That gap is what this studio was built to close.

---

### 4. What we do
*Register: Discovery framing. The logic of the offer, not a service menu.*

**Heading:**

> Built once. Run every week.

**Intro line:**

> Two ways of working. Most engagements start with the first and settle into the second.

**Launch**

> We start with what you're actually trying to say — the brief is rarely the real brief, and finding the real one is the work. From there: positioning, a voice that's recognisably yours, a visual identity, and a website built as a fast, static site. Nothing to update, nothing to break, nothing you'll be invoiced for maintaining.
>
> You review every stage in one place and nothing moves forward without your sign-off. When it goes live, it's already set up to be run — not left.

**Run**

> A monthly engagement that keeps your presence moving. A planned calendar, designed posts, edited clips, captions in your voice — every piece checked against your rules before you ever see it, and every piece approved by you before it goes anywhere.
>
> Your website stays alive the same way. Need the wording changed, a price updated, a treatment added? Ask for it, approve it, and it's live — no ticket queue, no small-change invoice.
>
> Building toward a launch, an event, a season? A Run can be shaped entirely around it.

---

### 5. How it works
*Register: Discovery framing → one editorial beat at the end. This is the system section — told as the client's experience, not the machinery.*

**Heading:**

> One place to see everything. One gate everything passes through.

**Body:**

> Behind the studio is a production system we built for ourselves, because the run side of this work is only affordable if the mechanical parts are automated and the judgment parts never are.
>
> What you see of it is simple. Every piece of work — pages, posts, clips, copy changes — lands in one private review space, on one link, behind your login. You approve it, ask for a change, or flag it for a conversation. From your desk or your phone.
>
> Sign-off isn't a courtesy. It's the gate the system enforces. Nothing ships without it — and everything that ships is exactly what you approved.

**The honesty paragraph (AI, locked framing):**

> The tools have changed what's possible. A studio of one can now plan, produce, and publish at a pace that used to take a team. But the judgment about what to make, what it should say, and whether it's good enough hasn't changed hands — and here, it never will. A person picks the moment. A person signs the words. The system just makes sure the rest keeps up.

## Quality check (copywriting skill) — run 12 July 2026

- First sentences start on the thing: ✓ (hero opens on the reader's knowledge, not the studio)
- Empathy before capability: ✓ (sections 2–3 before any offer)
- Active voice, no hedges: ✓
- Ending closes with weight: ✓ ("If what you're building matters to you, it matters here." kept as the About close — it still earns its place)
- No buzzwords: "end-to-end", "holistic", "solutions" absent; "system" used deliberately and defined by what the client sees
- Honesty constraints from plan §8: ✓ (no auto-publish claim, no deploy track-record claim, no client counts)
- Repetition watch: "one place" appears in hero, §5, and About ("one system") — deliberate as the spine of the page, but check for fatigue at prototype stage
- Read aloud: hero and §3 scan clean; §5 heading is long — acceptable as a two-beat, revisit in layout
