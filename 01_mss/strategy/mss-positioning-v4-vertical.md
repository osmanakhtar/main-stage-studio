## 1. The bet

v3 correctly moved MSS from "brand studio" to "presence partner" and from project economics to retainer economics. This document takes the next decision v3 left open: **which market MSS bets the company on.**

The decision (founder-selected, 23 Jul):

> **Build the flagship story on the (anonymised) combat sports content operation, because it is the deepest moat and the strongest proof. Aim the actual retainer sales at clinics and medical aesthetics, where budgets clear a real retainer without wincing. The sports machine is the credibility that makes the clinic sale easy.**

One sentence of positioning logic: *"I run the content operation for a live fight promotion, solo. Your clinic is not harder than that."*

## 3. ICP v4

**Primary buyer (who the site sells to):** owner-operated clinics and private practices with real revenue and a run-rate problem, not a form problem. Aesthetics, dental, dermatology, cosmetic, private GP, physio, veterinary. They have patients, budget, and recurring, compliance-sensitive content needs, and no sane way to keep the content running in-house.

Why this wallet:
- Recurring content demand is structural (treatments, offers, seasonality, before/after, education), not a one-off launch.
- High customer LTV means a £2–4k/mo retainer is a rounding error against one retained patient cohort.
- Compliance is a moat you already built: POM-grade lint exists. Clinics fear getting the regulated-content rules wrong; a pipeline that checks every post is a real, ownable safety promise.

**Flagship proof (what earns the sale):** the anonymised combat sports operation. It is the hardest content-ops problem you have solved (events, buildup arcs, media days, roster, coverage), run solo, on a repeatable system. It proves the machine can carry a workload no clinic will ever match.

**Values filter (survives from v1/v3):** intent over industry. A selection principle, never positioning copy.

## 4. Positioning

**Hero line (keep from v3):**
> You don't need a bigger team. You need a better system.

It survives the vertical narrowing intact, which is a good sign it's the right line.

**The proof move (new, v4):** the site's centre of gravity is not a claim, it is a demonstration. The felt problem ("we can't keep the content going and hiring is too expensive") is answered by showing an operation that already runs at a scale the buyer will never need.

**The differentiator promise (sharpened):** *the system does the labour, you keep the moment.* This is straight out of the hard-won lesson that machine-picked clips come out "off" even with frame verification. It is true, and it is the cleanest separation from every "AI content" competitor whose output feels like slop: the machine does the volume, a human keeps the judgment.

**The AI story (unchanged from v3):** method, not message. One honest paragraph. The live layer and Stage demonstrate it; the copy does not sell it.

## 5. Offer architecture — three layers, laddered labour → leverage

v3 built one revenue mechanic (retainers + occasional builds). v4 stacks three, deliberately. Layer 1 pays the bills. Layer 3 is the flagship. Layer 2 is the only layer that ever breaks the founder-hours ceiling.

### Layer 1 — Productised packages (live now, the earner)
Fixed scope, fixed price, systematised delivery. Not bespoke retainers. Bespoke forces you to reinvent scope per client and quietly caps margin; packages let the system carry delivery.

Concrete clinic packages:
- **Launch** — brand + Astro site + voice, fixed-scope build, architected to hand into a monthly. (v3's Launch, unchanged in shape.)
- **Clinic Monthly** — planned calendar across the clinic's treatment pillars, designed posts + edited clips + captions in voice, every piece compliance-checked, all reviewed and approved in Stage, site copy edits flowing to the live site. Proposed band £2,000–£4,000/mo (founder decision; anchored on patient LTV, not the sports rate).
- **Campaign** — time-boxed sprint around a launch, new treatment, or season. Also the natural trial that converts to a Monthly.

### Layer 2 — Vertical product tier (later, the ceiling-breaker)
Once the clinic Monthly is running for two or three clients, Stage + the content pipeline becomes a lighter-touch, self-serve "content ops for clinics" tier, with the human layer optional. This is what v3 called premature. It is premature *horizontally*. Inside a vertical where the tooling is already shaped for exactly this buyer, it is the only path off the founder-hours ceiling. Not built now; named now so the packages are designed to feed it.

### Layer 3 — Anchor rev-share / flagship (the proof engine)
The sports operation. Do not let it stay a thin weekly wage that also anchors your pricing downward. Where MSS is effectively the outsourced media department, price on outcome and take a slice of the upside, not a flat rate. Its real job in the business is Layer 3: it is the flagship that makes Layers 1 and 2 sellable, and it earns its keep as proof even before it earns its keep as revenue.

## 6. Pricing logic

- **Clinic retainers anchor on value:** one retained patient cohort dwarfs a £3k/mo retainer. Sell against the cost and risk of hiring, and against the cost of going dark.
- **The sports rate never appears in a clinic proposal.** Different market, different anchor. v3 §9 item 5 (£400/wk as retainer evidence) is explicitly retired for clinic pricing.
- **Packages beat hourly and beat bespoke.** Fixed price protects margin and makes delivery a system problem, not a negotiation.

## 8. Honest risks

1. **Dual audience for a solo operator.** Flagship in sports, sales in clinics is two worlds to hold in one head. Mitigation: the sports work is *proof*, not a serviced second market; only clinics get active sales effort. If it starts costing real delivery hours on both sides, collapse to clinics-only.
2. **Anonymised proof is weaker than a named logo.** "A live fight promotion" persuades less than a brand a buyer recognises. Mitigation: lead with concrete artefacts and numbers (assets produced, turnaround, roster size) rather than the name; a real, named clinic case study is the priority Work asset because it is the one you *can* name.
3. **Regulated-content liability.** Selling compliance-checked clinic content makes MSS adjacent to medical advertising rules (ASA/CAP, POM restrictions). The lint reduces risk; it does not remove liability. Needs a clear scope-of-responsibility line in the contract before this is sold as a safety claim.
4. **Layer 2 may never arrive.** If the product tier stalls, the business stays capped at founder hours. That is survivable (it is still a good solo income) but it is not a company. Layer 2 is the difference; design toward it from day one.
