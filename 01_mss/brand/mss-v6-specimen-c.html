02 · The mark

Spike, as a lens.

The same two corners as A and B. In C they sit on a pane of glass held over the client's work: the selection frame you edit inside.

Glass is only ever a control.

Nav, docks, toggles, toasts, captions. Never a content card, never a block of reading text.

Something real is always behind it.

Glass over a flat background is invisible. Put it over work, video or light.

Text on glass is tinted for the worst frame.

The glass carries its own scrim, and only full-strength type sits on it. Muted grey on glass fails over bright content (see the table).

It turns solid when asked.

Reduced transparency, or a browser without blur, gets opaque panels. Reduced motion freezes every loop.

03 · Day and night

One accent, two kinds of light.

Tungsten carries over from A, so C reads as an evolution, not a third brand. Night is the default for the tools; day is for reading, documents and anyone who prefers it. Use the switch at the top.
