# MSS Proposal Template

Reusable structure for any Main Stage Studio client proposal. First used for PureMed
Aesthetics, 26 September 2026:
`main-stage-studio/02_clients/puremed/puremed-engagement-proposal.md`

Proposals are written as local `.md` files in the client's own folder
(`02_clients/<client>/`), never as hosted Claude docs or artifacts.

Proposals are client-facing from the first draft (no internal notes, tooling or canon
references in the body). Write in the first person, to the client by name, in plain
words. Run the finished draft past `copy-reviewer` and, because it contains money,
`figures-reviewer` before sending.

---

## 1. Questions to answer before drafting

Work through these with the client or from discovery notes. Anything unanswered goes
into the draft as a recommendation and is flagged to Osman, never guessed silently.

**Scope and commitment**
- What is the firm commitment, and by what date? (Keep it small and certain.)
- What is the overall horizon (weeks or months) and the weekly time available?
- What comes after the commitment, in what order, and what does each stage depend on?
- What is explicitly out of scope?

**Discovery**
- Which discovery sessions have already happened, and what did they produce?
- Which session opens each remaining stage, and what written output follows it?

**Ways of working**
- Call cadence and length. Written update cadence and format.
- How sign-off happens (Stage review, email) and the client's review turnaround.
- Reply times, and what "urgent" means for this client.

**Commercials**
- The comparable market price, by deliverable, with sources checked on the day
  (see section 3).
- The client's price: fixed fee, monthly, pilot or discounted rate, and why.
- What happens after the build (retainer, running costs at cost, notice).
- Payment method, dates, and who pays third-party tools directly.

**Ownership and data**
- Who owns the domain, content and data. What MSS retains (the shared platform) and
  what the client gets on exit.
- Is personal or health data involved? If yes: controller/processor roles, DPA, DPIA
  before any record moves, UK hosting.

**Outstanding inputs**
- Access and documents needed this week, and those needed before later stages.

## 2. Section structure

| # | Section | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Summary | Firm commitment and date, horizon, market value versus client price. Three short paragraphs. |
| 2 | What I heard | The problem in the client's own terms. Bullets per system or pain point. |
| 3 | Scope | Committed items (what they get, when, go-live on sign-off), roadmap table (stage, what you get, depends on), not included. |
| 4 | Order of work and timeline | Weeks, dates, focus, Committed or Target. Why this order. How any cutover is protected. |
| 5 | Discovery sessions | One per stage: when, what we cover, what you get back. Nothing built until the summary is confirmed. |
| 6 | Ways of working | Cadence table, time commitment on both sides, reply times, decisions in writing. |
| 7 | Investment | Comparable price table with sourced references, other reference points, client rate table, how to pay. |
| 8 | Terms of engagement | Standard terms below, adjusted per client. |
| 9 | What I need from you | Checklists: this week, and before later stages. |
| 10 | Acceptance | Reply-to-accept or signature table. |
| 11 | Sources | Every pricing source, opened (not a search snippet), with the date checked. |

## 3. Pricing method

- Price by **deliverable**, not by hours. AI-assisted delivery makes hours understate
  value, and an hourly figure sets a precedent that is hard to move.
- Anchor each deliverable to **freelance and small-studio** market ranges, not agency
  ranges. That is the market MSS competes in.
- Open every source page and check the figure against it before quoting. Record the
  date checked. Drop any source whose page does not actually state the figure.
- Show the comparable total, then the client's price. For a discounted or family rate,
  label the discount plainly. For a full-price client, delete the discount line and the
  comparable table becomes the quote.
- Keep a day-rate reference as a sanity check only.

## 4. Standard terms

Adjust dates, notice and data clauses per client. Keep the wording plain.

1. **Term.** Build period dates, then month to month. 30 days' written notice either side.
2. **What's committed.** Only the named committed items are firm. Roadmap stages and dates are targets.
3. **Changes.** New requests go onto the roadmap; order can change at any progress call; flag anything that extends the horizon before starting.
4. **What you own.** Domain, content, photographs, all personal data, and anything built specifically for the client.
5. **What I own.** The shared underlying platform, licensed for as long as they are a client; on exit, site files and a full data export in standard formats within 14 days.
6. **Data protection.** Client is controller, MSS is processor; DPA signed and DPIA done before personal or health records move; UK hosting.
7. **Your responsibilities.** Accuracy and regulatory compliance of the client's own claims; reviews within five working days.
8. **Support.** Hosting, updates, security, backups; no guaranteed response time unless a paid support tier says otherwise.
9. **Liability.** Reasonable skill and care; capped at fees paid; no indirect loss; nothing limits what law does not allow to be limited.
10. **Confidentiality.** Kept confidential; general description of the work only with permission; never patient or customer data.

These terms are a plain-English starting point, not legal advice. Have a solicitor review
them once before using them with a full-price client.
