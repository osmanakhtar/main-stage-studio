## In the founder's own words

"I feel like identity is something that is ultra complex. I'm British, born to Pakistani immigrant parents, living a life in British culture whilst having Pakistani and Islamic values that were not really who I was. My entire life I've felt like I needed to find my own identity, and it's something I've been seeking for a long time.

Where I am today: I'm successful in my career, I have a family, I have my own worldview on politics, religion, faith, who I am, who I want to be, how I'll raise my kids. All of that has meant that I'm not conforming to a specific pattern of how we need to live. What I'm doing is defining what feels right for me and what represents me in the things that I do.

Why did I create Main Stage? I had a realisation that I do have a skill, and that skill is fairly technical. It's analytical. It's rooted in structure and thought, and it's always been kept in check by regulation and the rigour of working in big corporates. What I wanted to do is apply that framework to expression.

I think the conversations I've had with clients have shown that people feel this. People feel like they are slightly trapped in their own minds because they have an idea and they need to express it. Whether it's the technology, whether it's the day-to-day, it all gets lost in the noise. Even in my own life I've felt that — having responsibilities as a parent, as a husband, as a son. These things tend to take over the things that you feel and want.

Once I realised I've got this skill and this passion for expression, I'm trying to offer that to other people who want to either create something or grow something that has come from their own mind."

## On cultural influence and non-conformism

"My influences were popular culture — what we were exposed to through normal forms of media. But at around twelve or thirteen, I discovered hip hop for the first time. That had a massive impact on my view of challenging perspective, not taking yes for an answer or no for an answer, and having the right to speak and the right to express.

Hip hop culture is quite deep in the sense that it is non-conformist, yet that voice is a really important voice that needs to be listened to. It draws parallels to how I perceive justice. You can't dismiss something because it's a voice that doesn't deserve to be heard.

I was told: go to school, go to college, go to university. I've done all of that. But along the way, that wasn't really for me. I always felt let down by the education system. I didn't feel I fit in because I was doing things that would make me successful in my career, but what I wanted was to be expressive.

I'm at the point now where I can get back to expressing how I feel, with the skills that I've got."

---

## On AI as a thinking partner

"The key thing is being able to think about AI output critically. It's easy to accept everything AI says. Being able to challenge that output, to use specific skill sets, to think in a more creative way — that's been quite key. It's helped me challenge traditional thinking.

The differentiator is this is not AI slop. This is a craft that is leveraging AI to challenge my initial thinking, to get to a point which expresses me, my thoughts, and my values in the best way possible through the value proposition of the business.

The tooling has enabled the thinking to land in a clearer way. The tools didn't change the thinking. They removed the friction between the thought and the thing."

## What this means for MSS

The studio exists at the intersection of three things: rigorous analytical thinking, genuine creative expression, and the tools that remove the distance between the two.

The clients MSS serves are people at a similar intersection, whatever form it takes for them. They have something real. They haven't yet found the form to make it land. That gap is familiar territory.

The voice is non-conformist at its core. Not as a style choice. As a lived position. The confidence in every piece of MSS copy has a spine: a belief that the vision deserves to be heard regardless of where it comes from or what structures it has had to navigate.

That never appears on the surface. It runs underneath everything.
