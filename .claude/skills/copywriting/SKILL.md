# MSS Copywriting Skill

## What this skill does

Applies Main Stage Studio's craft principles to any copywriting task — for MSS itself or for
clients. The *how* of writing is constant. The *voice* adapts to whoever is in scope.

## Layer 1: Craft Principles

### Before you write

**Establish the context**
- Who is this for? (audience, not just client)
- Where does it live? (website, case study, proposal, social, editorial)
- What does it need to do? (inform, persuade, introduce, demonstrate)
- What register is right for this moment? (warm, direct, editorial, conversational)

**Find the real brief**
The brief is rarely the real brief. Before writing, identify:
- What is the copy actually trying to say?
- What does the reader need to feel or understand after reading it?
- What gets cut if you only have half the space?

The answer to the last question is usually the most important thing. Lead with it.

---

### How to construct copy

**Sentence-level rules**
- Every sentence earns its place. If it doesn't add something, cut it.
- Short sentences land harder. Use them for the point. Use longer sentences for the development.
- Rhythm matters. Read it aloud. If it stumbles, rewrite it.
- Active voice. Always.
- No throat-clearing. Start on the thing, not the approach to the thing.

**Structural rules**
- Lead with the reader's world, not the studio's process
- The first sentence is the hardest and the most important — it sets the register for everything that follows
- Transitions should be invisible. If you can feel the join, rewrite it.
- Endings should close with weight, not trail off

**What to cut**
- Adjectives doing the work that substance should do
- Hedges: "we think", "in our experience", "perhaps"
- Jargon dressed as meaning: "strategic discipline", "holistic approach", "end-to-end solution"
- Anything that sounds like it was written to sound good rather than to say something

## Layer 2: Voice

### MSS voice (for studio work)

**Character:** Quietly confident. Doesn't need to prove anything. Creative enough to earn
respect in that world. Structured enough to actually deliver.

**Three words:** Considered. Direct. Distinctive.

**What it never sounds like:**
- Fluffy — no passion without substance
- Arrogant — no "most studios do this wrong"
- Unsure — no excessive hedging
- Corporate — no passive voice, no buzzword stacking

**For the full MSS voice reference** → `mss-tone-of-voice.md`

### Client voice (for client work)

Load the client's tone of voice file before writing anything. The craft principles in Layer 1
apply — the voice, character, and examples come from the client file.

If no tone of voice file exists for the client, surface this before proceeding. Writing without
a voice reference produces generic copy.

---

## Quality check — run before delivering

Before any copy leaves a session, check against this list:

- [ ] Does the first sentence start on the thing, not the approach to the thing?
- [ ] Does every sentence earn its place?
- [ ] Is it in active voice throughout?
- [ ] Have hedges and qualifiers been cut?
- [ ] Does it lead with the reader's world, not the studio's process?
- [ ] Does the ending close with weight?
- [ ] Does it sound like the voice it's meant to sound like — not generic AI copy?
- [ ] Read it aloud — does it stumble anywhere?

---

## Notes for Claude

This is a craft skill, not a style guide. The principles exist to produce copy that means
something and sounds like it was written by a person with a point of view. Apply judgment —
these are not mechanical rules.

When in doubt: shorter, plainer, more direct.
