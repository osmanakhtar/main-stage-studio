# Main Stage Studio: Tone of Voice

*Worked examples by context: `copy-contexts.md`. The founder vision (`mss-founder-vision.md`) informs the voice and is never published.*

## Character

**Plain. Specific. A bit of swagger.**

Confident, direct and on the reader's side. The empathy is real, but it shows in what MSS does (hands over control, publishes prices, writes in plain English), not in long sentences about feelings. The non-conformist spine from the founder vision is allowed on the surface now: MSS has opinions and says them.

---

## Principles

1. **Name the thing.** Say what they get, in nouns. "A 10-page site you can edit yourself", not "a digital presence".
2. **Show the receipt.** Dates, page counts, what was built, what it replaced. A number beats an adjective. Every number passes the figures gate (`make figures`) before it is published.
3. **Talk like the owner.** First person, "I", because it is one person. "We" only when it genuinely means the studio and the client together.
4. **Hand over the mic.** Clients speak in their own words on case studies. MSS does not narrate other people's feelings.
5. **Short wins.** If a line works at half the length, use half.
6. **Point of view, out loud.** Agencies that keep the keys, rented websites, AI slop: MSS has a view and states it, without sneering at anyone.
7. **Only say what the screen shows.** In clips, demos and case studies, every claim must be visible or checkable. No "approve your posts here" over a screen that does not exist.

## The vocabulary

A light stage vocabulary for the **process**, never for what someone buys:

| Plain step | Brand name |
|---|---|
| Discovery call | Soundcheck |
| Prototype review on Stage | Rehearsal |
| Client sign-off | Your cue |
| Launch | Showtime |
| Monthly running | The run |

Products keep plain names: Review, Identity, Website, Campaign, Content, Platform, Care. Drop the stage words wherever they would sound twee.

## Never sounds like

- **Agency-speak:** "solutions", "end-to-end", "elevate", "unlock", "seamless", "transformative".
- **False scale:** "our team", "our experts".
- **Hedging or fake urgency:** "we think maybe", "limited spots", "book now".
- **Anything that would embarrass a regulated client** if they forwarded it to their compliance person.
- **Em dashes.** Standing rule for every piece of writing: use a comma, colon, full stop or brackets. En dashes in ranges (3–5 weeks) are fine.

## What stays private

The founder is visible as a professional, as **Os Akhtar**: name, photo, career in outline, point of view. Employers are never named. Family, faith and anything from the founder vision stay out of published copy.
