**Prepared for:** Shak and Rajan

**Status:** Draft for discussion

## 1. Purpose

You are setting up a new business selling saunas and plunge pools, dropshipped from your supplier, and you have asked for a website with Shopify behind it to handle checkout and orders, plus business email. This paper sets out the realistic ways to build the store, what each one costs to build and to run, and what each one means for you and your virtual assistant day to day. It ends with a recommendation.

Pure Earth Essentials has a Shopify store, with Amazon handling most of its orders, so this paper focuses on what is different here: high-value, installed products, and orders that pass to a supplier for delivery.

Three options are compared:

- **Option A: Shopify store.** A Shopify theme, designed and customised for your brand. Shopify runs the shop, checkout, orders and payments.
- **Option B: Custom-built store.** A site and shop built from scratch by Main Stage Studio, with Stripe taking payments and a custom order system behind it.
- **Option C: Custom front, Shopify behind.** A custom-built website for the brand experience, with Shopify running products, checkout and orders in the background.

---

## 2. What the store has to do

Whichever option is chosen, the store needs to handle:

- A small range of high-value products (roughly £2,900 to £5,800) with options such as size, heater type and add-ons.
- Secure card payments, plus finance options such as Klarna or PayPal Credit, which matter at these prices.
- Order confirmation, delivery and installation updates by email.
- Pre-purchase questions: site suitability, access, ground base and electrical supply.
- Orders passed to your supplier for delivery, with tracking and delivery dates coming back to the customer. Ideally automatic, not retyped.
- High-value orders checked (suitability, fraud) before they go to the supplier, because with dropship the product ships before any payment dispute would surface.
- Day-to-day running by your virtual assistant, with their own login and only the permissions they need.
- Discount codes, refunds, VAT invoices and customer records.
- Marketing emails: welcome, abandoned basket, aftercare and review requests.
- A way for you to update products, prices and content without a developer.

Most of this list is standard shop functionality. That point drives the comparison below.

### Before any build: name, brand and prototype

Whichever option you choose, the work starts the same way. First the name and brand, then a clickable prototype of the site that you review and refine before anything is built. This first phase can run while you finish agreeing terms with the supplier. The store build follows once those terms are signed, because the supplier's process shapes how orders, delivery and returns are set up.

### A new, separate store

We recommend a new Shopify store for the new business, not an addition to the Pure Earth account. It keeps the brands, finances, customer data and VAT separate, and lets the new company own its own store. What you know of Shopify from Pure Earth carries across.

## 3. The options in detail

### Option A: Shopify store

Your store runs on Shopify. We design and customise a theme to your brand, set up products, payments, shipping, finance, emails and legal pages, and train you to run it.

**Pros**
- **Proven checkout.** Shopify's checkout is secure and familiar to buyers, and it handles payment security for you.
- **Finance built in.** Klarna and PayPal can be switched on without custom development.
- **You can run it yourself.** Products, orders, refunds, discounts and customers are all managed in one admin designed for non-technical owners.
- **No lock-in to us.** Any Shopify developer can work on the store in future. You own the account and your data, and products, customers and orders can be exported.
- **Fastest to launch.** A realistic route to being live for the January peak in sauna and cold plunge demand.
- **Extensible.** Reviews, bundles, delivery date booking and email marketing are available as apps, without building them.
- **Familiar ground.** You and your VA already use a Shopify admin for Pure Earth, and Shopify is widely documented, so there is little new to learn.
- **Supplier connection.** Your supplier also runs on Shopify. If they support Shopify Collective, their products, prices and stock can sync into your store and orders can route to them automatically. To confirm with the supplier.
- **Staff accounts.** Your VA gets their own login with limited permissions and two-step verification, and Shopify's fraud analysis flags risky orders before they are passed on.

**Cons**
- **Ongoing monthly fees** for Shopify and any apps, for as long as the store runs.
- **App creep.** Each extra feature tends to be another monthly app subscription.
- **Design and journey limits.** Themes are flexible, but very bespoke journeys (a guided product finder, a detailed site survey before purchase) need apps or careful custom work.
- **Checkout customisation is limited** on standard plans. The checkout looks like Shopify's, with your branding applied.

### Option B: Custom-built store

We build the whole site and shop from scratch: product pages, basket, checkout through Stripe, an order and customer system, emails and an admin area for you.

**Pros**
- **Total design freedom.** Every page and step of the journey can be shaped around an advice-led sale.
- **No platform subscription.** You pay hosting and payment fees, not a monthly shop platform fee.
- **Lower card fees on standard cards.** Stripe charges 1.5% + 20p on standard UK cards, against Shopify's 2% + 25p on the Basic plan.

**Cons**
- **Rebuilds standard features.** Basket, checkout, stock, discounts, refunds, VAT invoices, abandoned basket emails and an order admin all have to be built and tested. This is where most of the cost and risk sits.
- **The card fee saving is smaller than it looks.** Stripe charges 2.8% + 20p on premium UK cards (most rewards and business credit cards). At £3,000 to £6,000 per order, many buyers will use credit cards, so the real blended rate can match or exceed Shopify's.
- **Ongoing dependence on us.** A bespoke system needs a developer who knows it. Maintenance, security updates and fixes are a permanent cost, not optional.
- **Longer to launch.** It is unlikely to be ready, fully tested, for January.
- **Responsibility for a live payment and order system** sits with the developer, so it needs a separate support and responsibility agreement before work starts.
- **Your team learns a bespoke admin.** Your VA would move from the Shopify admin they know to a one-off system, with no outside documentation or support.
- **No supplier sync.** Shopify Collective is not available, so the supplier connection has to be built and maintained.

### Option C: Custom front, Shopify behind

We build a custom website for the brand experience, and connect it to Shopify, which still runs products, checkout, payments and orders.

**Pros**
- **Brand freedom where it matters,** on the pages people browse, with Shopify's proven checkout and admin behind it.
- **Fast, distinctive site** without rebuilding shop features.
- **A sensible later upgrade** from Option A, if the brand outgrows a theme.

**Cons**
- **Two systems to run and pay for:** Shopify plus the custom website's hosting and care.
- **Many Shopify apps assume a Shopify theme** and do not work on a custom front, so some features need custom integration.
- **More build effort than Option A,** and ongoing developer support is needed for the front end.
- **Supplier sync may not fully apply.** Collective and order routing still run in Shopify, but how synced products display on a custom front needs checking.

**Bank transfer:** on all three options, offering bank transfer or Pay by Bank for full payment or deposits can cut fees noticeably at these order values.

## 6. Recommendation

**Option A, a Shopify store, designed and customised for the brand.**

- It does everything on the list in section 2 without rebuilding standard shop features.
- It is the lowest three-year cost and the fastest route to the January peak.
- You stay in control, and are not tied to one developer.
- The custom option's main saving (lower card fees) mostly disappears at these order values, because many buyers will pay with premium credit cards.
- You and your VA already know the Shopify admin, and your supplier is on Shopify, which opens the most direct route to automatic order handoff.

Where the journey needs something bespoke, such as a site suitability check or consultation booking, we build it within Shopify as forms and flows rather than replacing the platform.

**Option C stays open as a later step.** If, after launch, the brand needs a more distinctive website than a theme allows, we can build a custom front end and keep Shopify behind it, without migrating products, customers or orders.

**Option B is not recommended for launch.** It costs more, takes longer and leaves you dependent on a bespoke system, for features Shopify already provides.

## 7. What we need to confirm before quoting

1. The supplier terms, once agreed: margin, pricing rules, branding, and who handles delivery, installation, returns and warranty.
2. Whether the supplier supports Shopify Collective, or how else orders reach them and tracking comes back.
3. When the new company will be formed, and the name it will trade under.
4. Your VA's role, working hours and the access they need.
5. Number of products and options at launch.
6. Which finance options you want (Klarna, PayPal Credit, or both).
7. Whether customers buy outright, pay a deposit, or enquire first.
8. Expected order volume in the first year, to choose between Shopify Basic and Grow.
9. How many email mailboxes you need, and whether you prefer Google or Microsoft.
10. Which marketing emails are needed at launch.
