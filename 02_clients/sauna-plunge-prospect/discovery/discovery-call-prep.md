## 1. Who they are: Pure Earth Essentials

> **Correction after the call (17 Oct 2026):** Pure Earth sells mainly through Amazon, which handles fulfilment; it is not a Shopify dropship store, and it is being wound down. See `discovery-call-notes.md`.

### What the site shows (checked 16 Oct 2026)

- **Founders:** Rajan Sethi, Shakir Mughal and Nafisa Mughal, described as lifelong friends who ran dental practices. Idea started 2019.
- **Relationship (confirmed 16 Oct):** the returns address is White House Dental Studio, 34 High Street, Winslow, MK18 3HB, PureMed's building. Shak is married to Nafisa (PureMed), and Rajan is Shak's business partner. Shak and Rajan lead this engagement, not Nafisa. It's a warm referral inside an existing relationship, and the open PureMed items (email approvals, DPIA re-sign) sit in the background.
- **Platform:** Shopify, Prestige theme version 4.12.2 (a premium theme, several major versions out of date). Judge.me reviews, Pinterest tag, Google tag. No dropship or email marketing app is visible from the storefront, but back-office apps don't show publicly.
- **Catalogue:** 58 products, all sold under the Pure Earth Essentials brand: bamboo toothbrushes, floss, copper tongue scrapers, skincare, massage candles, organic cotton clothing, gift packs. Prices mostly £3.49 to £22.99, with bulk "wholesale" duplicates of many lines at £27 to £64.
- **Activity:** newest product added February 2024. Product records were updated today, so something (an app or the VA) is still touching the store, but the range hasn't grown in over two and a half years.
- **Reviews:** low counts (4 to 8 on the hero products).
- **Operations as published:** dispatch within 2 to 3 business days; free UK shipping over £50; 30-day returns, customer pays return postage, returns go to the dental practice; missing-item claims need an unedited unboxing video within 7 days.
- **Contact:** a Gmail address (pure.earth.essent@gmail.com) and a contact form.
- **Legal details:** no registered company name, number, VAT number or trading address anywhere on the site.

### What that tells us

1. **They already run a Shopify dropship business.** Shopify isn't a guess on their part. They know the admin, and they'll expect the new store to run the same way, with the VA handling orders.
2. **The model doesn't transfer as is.** Pure Earth is £5 to £50 parcels, a small number of products, low stakes per order. The new business is £3,000 to £6,000 installed products where one order is worth more than months of toothbrushes. Delivery booking, site checks, installers on a customer's property, finance, fraud and returns of a 300kg item are all new problems the current setup has never handled.
3. **Professional basics are missing.** Gmail contact, no company details, an out-of-date theme and a static catalogue suggest nobody owns the store's upkeep. For a £4,000 purchase, buyers check who they're dealing with. This is the most likely meaning of "they need emails": domain mailboxes, not just order emails.
4. **Their current returns and claims terms won't work** for installed products, and parts of them (an unboxing video as a condition) would read badly to a high-value buyer.
5. **There is a natural service gap:** documenting how the VA runs the operation. At Pure Earth that knowledge probably sits in the VA's head.

## 2. The supplier: Outdoor Living 365

Halifax retailer (Horley Green Road, HX3 6AS, Co. no. 11033925). SaunaPlunge is its own hero range.

- **Products:** traditional barrel and cabin saunas (Pennine Barrel, Bronte), infrared saunas indoor and outdoor (Yorkshire Cabin, Dales Glow), the Peak Plunge ice bath with chiller, plus accessories.
- **Retail prices:** Bronte £2,900; Yorkshire Cabin infrared £2,900 (2 person) and £3,900 (4 person); Pennine Barrel 6 person £5,800.
- **They already sell direct to consumers** on Shopify, with Klarna and PayPal Credit, a garden showroom, and their own delivery and installation.
- **Trade programme (SaunaPlunge only):** wholesale (bulk, minimum orders) or dropship (they ship to your customer). Specs, installation guides and marketing assets provided. Margins, pricing rules, rebranding, territory and warranty terms are not published. Contact: trade@outdoorliving365.co.uk.
- **They also run an affiliate programme.**
- **They're on Shopify, so Shopify Collective may be an option:** it syncs a supplier's products, prices and stock into a retailer's store and routes orders back automatically. Around 1,900 UK stores use it; UK eligibility is not confirmed by Shopify officially.

### The question that shapes everything

The client would sell the same products as the supplier, who already sells them direct at published prices with finance, a showroom and installation. **Why would a customer buy from them instead?** Without a good answer, the site becomes a copy of the supplier's store on a thinner margin, and their ads bid against the supplier on the same product names.

Possible answers to listen for:

1. **Own brand or white-label:** sold under their brand, not as SaunaPlunge.
2. **Territory:** an area the supplier serves poorly (the South, Scotland, Wales).
3. **Full-service install:** survey, base, electrician, delivery booking, handover.
4. **Bundles and advice:** a curated home recovery setup with honest guidance.
5. **Commercial buyers:** gyms, physios, hotels, holiday lets, sports clubs.
6. **Their own audience:** a dental and aesthetics clientele (PureMed, the practice) is a warm, wellness-minded, higher-income audience. This could be a real advantage.

If the honest answer is "none", the affiliate programme (commission, no liability) is worth putting next to dropship.

## 5. Question bank

Must-ask questions are marked ★.

### 5.1 Pure Earth today
- ★ How does an order flow today, from checkout to the customer's door? Who is the supplier, and how do orders reach them (app, email, spreadsheet)?
- ★ What does the VA do, day to day? Orders, customer emails, refunds, product listings, social?
- What's worked, and what's been painful? Why has the range stayed the same since 2024?
- Rough volumes: orders a month, revenue, return rate. (Context only; don't push.)
- Which Shopify plan and apps do they pay for? Who has the account owner login?
- Is Pure Earth staying as it is, being wound down, or folded into the new business?
- Is it a limited company? Will the new business be a separate company?

### 5.2 The people and the new business
- Shak and Rajan lead (confirmed). ★ How do they split roles and decisions, and who signs off on brand and spend? Is Nafisa involved at all?
- ★ The company doesn't exist yet: who's forming it, when, and will it be separate from Pure Earth?
- Is it alongside their other work, or someone's main focus?
- What does success look like at 6 and 12 months?

### 5.3 The supplier deal
- ★ Where are the supplier talks? (In progress, nothing signed.) What has the supplier said so far, and what's still open?
- ★ Margin or trade discount, and payment terms: pay per order, or on account?
- ★ Pricing rules: can they discount, bundle or undercut the supplier's own prices?
- ★ Branding: white-label, or sold as SaunaPlunge? Can they use the name in their domain or ads?
- **Blind shipping:** will paperwork and the delivery or install team carry the supplier's brand?
- **No poaching:** will the supplier agree not to market to customers whose details they receive?
- Territory or exclusivity: can other dropshippers sell the same range?
- Order handoff: Shopify Collective, an app, email or a portal? How do tracking and delivery dates come back?
- Lead times, stock alerts, notice of price changes or discontinued lines.
- Who handles delivery booking, installation, damage in transit, returns and warranty claims? Is the supplier's warranty to them back to back with what they promise customers?
- Product data and images: supplied, in what format, and can they use the photography?

### 5.4 Customers and positioning
- ★ Who's the ideal customer? Do they plan to sell to their own practice patients and contacts?
- ★ Why buy from them, not the supplier? (Section 2.)
- National online, or a region first?
- Premium and advice-led, or value and fast?

### 5.5 How a sale and an order work
These are high-value, installed products. Walk through one order end to end.

- ★ Buy outright online, pay a deposit, or enquire first? Or both?
- ★ Pre-purchase checks: access, ground base, electrical supply. Who checks, before the order goes to the supplier?
- Finance: Klarna, PayPal Credit? (Simple buy-now-pay-later through the provider; anything more needs an FCA-authorised partner.)
- Delivery: kerbside or placed, two-person or crane, who books the slot with the customer?
- Installation and electrics: the supplier, a partner, or none? Outdoor electrical supplies usually need a registered electrician.
- Commercial enquiries: a separate quote route?

### 5.6 The VA and operations
- ★ Will the same VA run this business? Where are they, and what hours do they work?
- ★ Who answers the phone? Buyers spending £4,000 often want to talk to someone, often in UK evenings and weekends. Is that the VA, a founder, or nobody?
- How does the VA hand off to the supplier and escalate problems to the founders?
- Shopify access: does the VA use the owner login or their own staff account? (Own account, limited permissions and two-step verification is the safe setup, especially with refunds and payouts at these values.)
- Fraud: who reviews a high-value order before it's sent to the supplier? With dropship, the product ships before a chargeback would surface.
- Is any of the current process written down? Would they value documented procedures and email templates for the VA?
- Tools the VA uses beyond Shopify: Gmail, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, a helpdesk?

### 5.7 Brand
- ★ Is there a name? A logo? Anything they already like?
- Is the name checked (domain, Companies House, trademarks, social handles)?
- Should it connect to Pure Earth or the practices at all, or stand alone?
- Brand feel in three words: Scandinavian calm, athletic recovery, luxury spa, outdoor rugged?
- Imagery: supplier photos (shared with every other reseller), own photography, or generated lifestyle scenes? Product shots must stay true to the real product.

### 5.8 Website and Shopify
- ★ A new, separate Shopify store (recommended, separate brand and business), or added to the Pure Earth account?
- ★ Products and options at launch: sizes, heater types, add-ons, bundles.
- Pages: about, how it works, delivery and install, finance, FAQs, buying guides, reviews, contact, trade.
- A product finder or comparison?
- Early reviews: supplier reviews, Trustpilot, Google?
- Who updates the site after launch: the VA, a founder, or us?
- Accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks)?

### 5.9 Emails
Pure Earth uses Gmail. For this business, confirm which of these they mean:
- ★ **Business email:** mailboxes on their own domain (Google Workspace or Microsoft 365), with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up so emails don't land in spam, plus a shared inbox the VA can work from.
- ★ **Order emails:** Shopify notifications rewritten for a long-lead, installed product (order received, supplier confirmed, delivery booked, installed, aftercare).
- ★ **Marketing emails:** welcome, abandoned basket, post-purchase care, review request, via Shopify Email or Klaviyo.

### 5.10 Money
- ★ VAT: at £4,000 an order, they cross the £90,000 threshold at around two orders a month. Will the new company be VAT registered from day one?
- **Payout holds:** Shopify Payments or Stripe may hold back payouts for a new store selling high-value goods with long delivery times. Can they cover paying the supplier before customer money lands?
- Card authorisations and Klarna expire after a set period. Payment is taken at order, delivery may be weeks later; this needs setting up deliberately.
- Separate business bank account for the new company?

### 5.11 Legal and compliance (raise lightly; MSS sets things up, it doesn't give legal advice)
- **Seller of record:** the customer's contract is with them, not the supplier. Faulty goods and the 14-day cancellation right are theirs to honour.
- **Business details on the site:** online sellers must show who they are: legal name, geographic address, contact email, and for a company its registered number and office. Pure Earth doesn't, today.
- **Insurance:** public and product liability as the seller of electrical heating products; installer insurance if installs are arranged.
- **Customer data:** a VA outside the UK handling customer data is an international transfer under UK GDPR, so it needs a written agreement. Passing customer details to the supplier needs one too. The privacy policy has to say both.
- **Health claims:** advertising rules (ASA/CAP) restrict claims that saunas or cold plunges treat conditions. Copy stays on feel, recover, relax.
- **Product safety:** confirm UKCA or CE marking with the supplier.
- **Returns terms:** written for a 300kg item, mirrored in the supplier agreement. No unboxing-video condition.

### 5.12 Budget, timeline, decision
- ★ Budget for the build, and for ongoing support.
- ★ Target launch and why. (January is the peak for saunas and cold plunges. Tight from mid October, possible with a focused scope.)
- Anyone else quoting? Or are they thinking of setting up the theme themselves, as with Pure Earth?
- How they like to work: calls, a shared review link, email, WhatsApp?

## 8. Watch-outs

- **They'll assume Pure Earth's playbook works here.** It mostly doesn't. Explain why with specifics (a £4,000 order, a delivery slot, an electrician), not as criticism of how they run Pure Earth.
- **Thin-margin trap:** small dropship margin, no differentiation, plus build fees and ads may not pay back. Say so.
- **Remote-only operations at high value:** fraud, payout holds and phone expectations are real risks with a VA-only setup. Offer the operations pack as the answer, not a lecture.
- **Existing-client dynamics:** Shak and Rajan are the clients here, not Nafisa. Keep scopes, invoices and approvals separate from PureMed, with a separate contract addressed to the new company (or to Shak and Rajan until it exists), and don't let PureMed's open items bleed into this one.
- **No company, no supplier deal yet:** don't let the build start before the supplier terms are signed. Brand and prototype can run while the deal closes; the Shopify build waits for it.
- **Supplier dependency:** product data, images and order flow depend on the supplier responding. Name it in the timeline.
- **January pressure:** scope Option A or B tightly, with a phase 2 after launch.
