## 1. What the supplier research tells us

Outdoor Living 365 is a Halifax retailer (Horley Green Road, HX3 6AS, Co. no. 11033925). SaunaPlunge is its own hero range.

- **Products:** traditional barrel and cabin saunas (Pennine Barrel, Bronte), infrared saunas indoor and outdoor (Yorkshire Cabin, Dales Glow), the Peak Plunge ice bath with chiller, plus accessories.
- **Retail prices on their site:** Bronte 1 to 2 person £2,900; Yorkshire Cabin infrared £2,900 (2 person) and £3,900 (4 person); Pennine Barrel 6 person £5,800.
- **They already sell direct to consumers** on what looks like a Shopify store (the `/collections/` and `/pages/` URL pattern), with Klarna and PayPal Credit, a garden showroom, and their own delivery and installation service.
- **Trade programme:** SaunaPlunge range only. Two routes: **wholesale** (bulk, discounted, minimum order quantities) or **dropship** (you sell, they ship to your customer). They provide specs, installation guides and marketing assets. The terms, margins, MOQs, price rules, white-label options and warranty responsibility are **not published**. Contact: trade@outdoorliving365.co.uk.
- **They also run an affiliate programme.**

### The question that shapes everything

The client would sell the same products as the supplier, who already sells them direct, at published prices, with finance, a showroom and installation. **What reason does a customer have to buy from the client instead?** If there is no good answer, the website becomes a nicer copy of the supplier's store with a thinner margin, and paid ads would bid against the supplier on the same product names.

Possible answers to listen for (or suggest):

1. **Own brand / white-label:** the products are sold under the client's brand, not as SaunaPlunge.
2. **Territory:** a region the supplier does not serve well (South, Scotland, Wales), with local survey, install and aftercare.
3. **Full-service install:** site survey, base or groundworks, electrician, delivery booking, handover. Traditional saunas need a dedicated electrical supply, which is a real customer pain point.
4. **Bundles and expertise:** a curated "home recovery setup" (sauna + plunge + base + accessories) with honest advice on choosing.
5. **Commercial buyers:** gyms, physios, hotels, glamping and holiday lets, sports clubs. Different sales motion (quotes, not checkout).
6. **Experience:** a showroom, demo days or a try-before-you-buy session.

How they answer decides the brand, the site structure, the Shopify setup and how big the proposal is.

## 3. Call agenda (45 to 60 minutes)

1. **Them** (10 min): who they are, why this business, why now.
2. **The model** (15 min): the supplier relationship and how they will differ (section 1). This is the most important part.
3. **Customers and sales** (10 min): who buys, how they buy, what happens after checkout.
4. **What they think they need** (10 min): website, Shopify, emails, brand. Let them describe it first.
5. **Practicalities** (5 to 10 min): budget, timeline, decision-maker, what they bring.
6. **Next steps** (2 min): what you will send and when.

Say up front that the aim is to understand the business, not just the website, because the website has to do the job the business needs.

## 4. Question bank

Must-ask questions are marked ★. Without them you cannot write a firm proposal.

### 4.1 The people and the business
- ★ Who is behind the business, and what is each person's role? Who makes the final decision and who pays?
- Background: wellness, fitness, trades, retail, or new to all of it?
- ★ Is the company registered? VAT registered (or will be soon, given the order values)?
- Is this full-time, or alongside other work? Who will run the store day to day?
- What does success look like at 6 and 12 months (units a month, revenue, a showroom)?

### 4.2 The supplier relationship
- ★ Trade or dropship? Have they applied, been approved, or signed terms?
- ★ What is the margin or discount? This decides how much marketing the business can afford and which services make sense.
- ★ Are there **minimum advertised price** rules? Can they discount, bundle or run sales?
- ★ Can they rebrand the products (white-label), or must they be sold as SaunaPlunge? Can they use the SaunaPlunge name in their domain, ads or keywords?
- Territory: is there any exclusivity, or could other dropshippers sell the same products?
- Product data: will the supplier supply images, specs, dimensions, weights and manuals? Is there a feed or API for stock and prices, or is it a spreadsheet and emails?
- Image rights: can they use the supplier's photography, or do they need their own?
- How does an order reach the supplier, and how do tracking and delivery dates come back?
- Who handles **delivery booking, installation, returns, damage in transit and warranty claims**? Whose name is on the warranty?
- Lead times and stock reliability. What happens if an item is out of stock after the customer pays?
- Will they sell anything beyond SaunaPlunge (other brands, accessories, their own products) now or later?

### 4.3 Customers and positioning
- ★ Who is the ideal customer? Homeowners, fitness enthusiasts, recovery-focused athletes, commercial buyers?
- ★ Why would someone buy from them rather than the supplier or Amazon? (Section 1.)
- Geography: national online, or a local area first?
- Price position: premium and advice-led, or value and fast?
- Any competitors or brands they admire, or want to avoid looking like?

### 4.4 How the sale works
These are high-value, heavy, installed products, so the "checkout" is only one part of the journey.

- ★ Do they expect people to buy straight online at £3k to £6k, or enquire first? Do they want both (buy now, plus "book a consultation")?
- Finance: Klarna, PayPal Credit, or a regulated finance partner? (Note: offering consumer credit beyond simple buy-now-pay-later needs FCA-authorised partners. Keep it with a provider.)
- Deposits or payment in full?
- ★ Pre-purchase checks: access, ground base, electrical supply, planning or building regs (mostly not needed for garden saunas, but customers ask). Do they want a site-survey or suitability form?
- Delivery: kerbside or placed? Crane or team lift for barrels? Who books the slot?
- Installation and electrics: offered by them, the supplier, or a partner electrician? Priced separately?
- Commercial quotes: do they want a trade or business enquiry route?

### 4.5 Brand
- ★ Is there a name? A logo? Any colours, fonts or ideas they already like?
- Is the name checked (domain, Companies House, trademarks, social handles)?
- Brand feel: three words. Scandinavian calm, athletic performance, luxury spa, outdoor rugged?
- Do they need a tone of voice and brand guidelines for future use (signage, van livery, social, showroom)?
- Photography: will they need lifestyle imagery of real installs, or can launch use supplier and generated scenes? (Product shots must stay true to the real product.)

### 4.6 Website and Shopify
- ★ Is Shopify decided because of a recommendation, or have they used it before? Any existing Shopify account?
- ★ Scope: how many products at launch, any variants (heater options, size, wood finish), add-ons and bundles?
- Pages they expect: about, how it works, delivery and install, finance, FAQs, guides (traditional vs infrared, cold plunge basics), reviews, contact, trade.
- Do they want a product comparison or "find your sauna" quiz?
- Reviews: any way to get early reviews (the supplier's, Trustpilot, Google)?
- Blog and SEO content: important to them now, or later?
- Who will edit the site after launch? How confident are they with Shopify?
- Any integrations: accounting (Xero, QuickBooks), CRM, live chat, WhatsApp, booking calendar?

### 4.7 Emails ("they need emails")
This could mean three different things. Clarify which:
- ★ **Business email:** mailboxes on their own domain (Google Workspace or Microsoft 365), e.g. hello@ and sales@. Setup, domain records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and a branded signature.
- ★ **Order emails:** Shopify's order confirmation, shipping, delivery and install updates, branded and rewritten for a long-lead, installed product.
- ★ **Marketing emails:** newsletter and automated flows (welcome, abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase care guide, review request, winback), via Shopify Email or Klaviyo.

### 4.8 Marketing and launch
- ★ Target launch date? (Demand for home saunas and cold plunges peaks around January, new year wellness, and through winter. A January launch is tight from mid October but possible for a focused scope.)
- Marketing budget after launch, per month? Paid ads (Meta, Google), social, influencers, local?
- Social accounts: exist, or need setting up?
- Do they want ongoing help after launch, or a handover?

### 4.9 Legal and compliance (raise lightly, it shows expertise)
- **Health claims:** advertising rules (ASA/CAP code) restrict claims that saunas or cold plunges treat or prevent conditions. Copy must stay on "feel, recover, relax" not medical outcomes.
- **Consumer rights:** 14-day cancellation for online sales applies, which matters for a 300kg barrel. Returns policy and terms need careful drafting (by them or a solicitor).
- **Data and marketing consent:** cookie consent, privacy policy, and opt-in for marketing emails (UK GDPR and PECR).
- **Product safety:** electrical products must carry UKCA or CE marking. Confirm with the supplier.
- Make clear MSS sets these up properly but does not give legal advice.

### 4.10 Budget, timeline, decision
- ★ Budget range for the build, and for ongoing monthly support. Is it fixed, or depends on what's included?
- ★ Hard deadline, and why (a launch event, a stock delivery, the January peak)?
- Are they talking to other agencies or freelancers? Or considering doing it themselves on a Shopify theme?
- How do they prefer to work: regular calls, a shared review link, email?

**Shopify flag:** the Website product was priced around MSS's own Astro build. A Shopify store carries extra work (product data import, variants, payments, shipping rules, finance apps, order notifications) plus the client's running costs (Shopify plan, apps, Klaviyo). Decide before quoting whether this is Website plus a scoped Shopify add-on, or a separate price. That is your call, not settled anywhere yet.

**Emails** (4.7) split across products: domain mailboxes and DNS setup fit Website or a small fixed add-on; branded order emails fit Website; marketing flows fit Campaign.

### Suggested proposal shape (three options)

- **Option A, Opening Night:** Identity plus Website (Shopify), branded order emails, domain email setup. For a fast or budget-led launch.
- **Option B, Opening Night plus Campaign (recommended):** Option A plus a launch campaign aimed at the January peak, with email flows and launch imagery.
- **Option C, Launch and run:** Option B plus Care, with Content monthly if the numbers support it. Platform noted as a later phase once the supplier mechanics are known and the clause exists.

Keep builds as fixed fees with phased payments (deposit, milestone, launch) and the monthly products separate.

## 6. Must-haves before writing the proposal

You can send a firm proposal once you know:

1. The business name, or whether naming is in scope.
2. The supplier model: trade vs dropship, white-label or SaunaPlunge branding, margin, price rules.
3. The differentiation: why customers buy from them, not the supplier.
4. Buy-online vs enquire-first, and who handles delivery, install and warranty.
5. Product count and variants at launch.
6. What "emails" means (business, order, marketing, or all three).
7. Launch date and the reason for it.
8. Budget range for the build and ongoing.
9. Who decides, and who will run the store after launch.

If 2 or 3 are unclear after the call, propose a short paid **discovery and brand strategy phase** first, then quote the build. That protects you from pricing a store for a business model that is still moving.

---

## 7. Watch-outs

- **Thin-margin trap:** if the dropship margin is small and there is no differentiation, a large build fee plus ad spend may not pay back for them. Be honest about this; it builds trust.
- **Scope creep from "it's a new business":** they may expect help with suppliers, accounts, legal and operations. Be clear what is in and out.
- **Supplier dependency:** product data, images and order flow depend on the supplier responding. Build their responsiveness into the timeline and name it as a dependency.
- **January deadline pressure:** if they want the January peak, scope Option A or B tightly with a phase 2 after launch.
