Font options — Art by Ayesha Johar

Three directions.
Pick the one that feels like her.

Each option shown in the arrival context — name on teal, italic Johar in gold — plus specimen text and character notes. The J is legible in all three.

Option 01 — Fraunces

Art by

Ayesha Johar

Beauty and resistance are one thing.

Warm · Soft · Expressive

Thirty years of painting what she believes in — not what she observes.

Character

Optical size serif. Soft, almost rounded. Warm and approachable. The italic has genuine personality without the historical quirks of older typefaces.

Best for

Work that wants to feel alive and made by hand. Less formal than Spectral. Closer to the energy of her acrylic paintings than the restraint of her pencil drawing.

Risk

Can feel slightly soft if not balanced with strong layout. Needs the teal and the ink to hold its weight.

Option 02 — Spectral

Art by

Ayesha Johar

Beauty and resistance are one thing.

Refined · Quiet · Long-form

Thirty years of painting what she believes in — not what she observes.

Character

Designed for screen reading. Light weight has real elegance. More restrained than Fraunces — built for long text as much as display.

Best for

If the site should feel like a long exhale — quiet, considered, the work doing all the talking. Good for an artist who has nothing to prove.

Risk

Can feel too refined for the political weight of the work. Witness and the Earth painting need more friction than Spectral naturally carries.

Option 03 — Lora

Art by

Ayesha Johar

Beauty and resistance are one thing.

Grounded · Readable · Balanced

Thirty years of painting what she believes in — not what she observes.

Character

Brushstroke origins. Contemporary but rooted. The italic J is clean and confident. Sits between the warmth of Fraunces and the restraint of Spectral.

Best for

Holds the full range of her work — the spiritual and the political and the intimate — without pulling too far in any one direction. The most versatile of the three.

Risk

More widely used than the other two. Less distinctive at first glance — though the context and colour system do enough to make it feel owned.
