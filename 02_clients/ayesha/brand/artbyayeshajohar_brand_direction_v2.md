## Discovery

### What we found in the work

Before any creative decisions were made, we looked carefully at your paintings. The mandalas, the Ganesha, the water studies, the Mansa Forts, the George Floyd portrait, the pencil drawing of your wife, the landscapes, the architectural pieces.

You paint what you believe in. Not only what you observe, but what you hold.

The range of your work, which might at first look like stylistic variation, is coherence at a deeper level. The mandala and the George Floyd portrait are not different modes. Both honour what should be inviolable.

Your answers gave us the other half of the through-line. The work is not only about the sacred and the violated. It is about **what is hidden in both**. Colours and shapes exist everywhere, unseen, and your paintings expose them. That is why the same work can hold beauty and discomfort at once, and why it is layered on purpose: the people who love your paintings say there is always something new to see.

And running through all of it, quieter than the rest: love. The pencil drawing of your wife sits differently to everything else. More private. More still. It still needs its own room.

## The name

### Art by Ayesha Johar

In your own words:

> Johar means jewel, courage and ultimate sacrifice.
>
> In 1600s Rajasthan, the women of a captured empire used to sacrifice themselves in a pit of fire rather than be shackled as sex slaves by the conquerors.
>
> Being a Johar, when my wife and I chose this name after we married, was a statement that we live by our principles, unapologetically and at all costs. I am political. I have been decolonising my mind and soul, and I am a justice fighter. Johar encompasses those values and passions.

*Your words, lightly edited for spelling and punctuation only. Please check we have kept your meaning exactly, and see the question about the date at the end.*

This changes how the name works in the identity. In version 1 we treated Johar as something quiet and interior: an essence. You have told us it is the opposite. It is a jewel, but it is also a refusal: principle held at any cost. It is a name that stands its ground.

That is not separate from the work. It is the same act. The mandala, the George Floyd portrait and the name you chose together all refuse to let something precious be taken.

**What this means for the design**

- Your statement replaces our definition in Movement III (Who you are). It is the anchor of that section, in your voice, not ours.
- "Johar" in gold italic remains the signature moment of the identity. Gold now reads as the jewel, and the fire, rather than only warmth.
- The name treatment stays: "Art by" small and receding, "Ayesha" at full weight, "Johar" last. You are centre stage.
- The Arabic script (جوهر) stays beside the name, as you asked. It now sits above your own statement rather than our dictionary definition.

## The line

### Beauty is a form of resistance.

In version 1 we proposed "Beauty and resistance are one thing." We arrived at it from the outside, by looking at your practice as a whole.

In your answers, you said it yourself:

> For me, beauty is a form of resistance. Hidden colours and shapes exist everywhere, and once I expose them in my paintings, it forces us to see beauty in both the good and the bad. Through that altered way of seeing, I urge the audience to see what we can reveal, and to challenge what is harmful in a different way.

The idea is the same. The difference is that this sentence is yours. We recommend using it as the line.

It appears once on the site, at the arrival, and never again. Its power is in its singularity. The full statement above can live in Movement III, where there is room for it.

## The work

### Five bodies of meaning

Most portfolio sites organise work by medium, date or category. We proposed something different: organising by meaning. Not what the paintings are made of or when they were made, but what they hold.

| Body of work | What it holds | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| **The Sacred** | Mandalas, Ganesha, geometric symmetry. Spiritual traditions held with devotion and curiosity. Work that opens rather than closes. | Teal, the deepest in the identity: interior, devotional, ritual. |
| **Witness** | George Floyd. The Earth with a bullet through it. Work that refuses to look away from what humanity does to itself and to the world. | Ember: resistance held with dignity. |
| **World** | Architecture, travel, the places that shaped you. Amsterdam, Mauritius, London. The built world as emotional landscape. | Near-black, for the weight of cities. |
| **Nature** | Ocean water, shorelines, palm trees, skies. The world before human damage. | Mauritian daylight blue: open and generous. |
| **Love** | The pencil drawing of your wife. The intimate work, given more space and a quieter register than everything else. | Gold: warm, personal, the most private body of work. |

Your answers sharpen what these bodies are for. Each one shows beauty in something, the good and the bad alike. Witness is the clearest case: the beauty is in the painting, not in what it depicts, and that is exactly the resistance you describe.

You have confirmed these five bodies of work and their names as they are.

## Visual direction

### Colour, type and atmosphere

Every visual decision was drawn from the work itself and from what we know of your life and practice. Two principles shaped the system: it must feel continuous with the work's emotional register, and it must be entirely distinct from any house style the studio building this site might otherwise reach for.

Your answers add a third: **the site must reward looking longer.**

### Colour

You told us colour is central, in the work and in how you put yourself together: an expansion of colours, set side by side. We have taken that seriously.

The six identity colours below stay, because each one has a direct source in your paintings. But their job is now clearer. They frame the work; they do not compete with it. The paintings carry the full range of colour. The site holds back so that your colour lands at full strength.

| Colour | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Deep teal | #0F3D35 | Arrival, hero |
| Teal | #1A6B5C | Signature colour, The Sacred |
| Ocean | #2A6E8A | Nature |
| Saffron | #B8620E | Accent, labels |
| Gold | #C98B1A | Johar, Love |
| Ember | #7A2810 | Witness |
| Ink | #1A1410 | Type, World |
| Canvas | #EDE5D8 | Background |
| Parchment | #F4EDE0 | Text on dark surfaces |
| Muted | #6E6154 | Secondary text (darkened from #8A7D6E so small text is easy to read) |

On small text, saffron and gold are used in slightly deeper shades on light backgrounds, and slightly lighter shades on dark ones, so every word stays easy to read. The colours themselves do not change.

Canvas (#EDE5D8) replaces pure white as the base. More pigmented, warmer, closer to the physical surface of the work. Never clinical.

### Typography

Two typefaces, chosen to feel entirely separate from the studio's own identity and true to your practice.

- **Fraunces** (display, headlines, the name). An optical-size serif. Warm, slightly rounded, with the quality of something made rather than designed. The italic "Johar" in gold is the signature moment of the identity.
- **DM Sans** (body, navigation, captions). Clean, warm and unpretentious. It sits under Fraunces without competing.

### Atmosphere: layers and depth

Your paintings are layered on purpose, and the people who love them keep finding something new. The site should behave the same way.

- **Detail views.** Every painting can be opened large and explored close up, so a visitor can find the colours and shapes that do not show at thumbnail size.
- **Slow reveals.** Details and passages of your own words surface as a visitor moves through the site, rather than all at once.
- **Room to look.** Generous space around each painting. Nothing crowds the work or hurries the eye.
- **Something to come back for.** A visitor who returns should feel there is more there than they saw the first time.

## Site structure

### Four movements

The site is organised as movements, like a piece of music. Each has its own register and purpose. The structure moves from immersion, through the work, to presence, to action.

**I. Arrival.** Full-viewport teal hero. The name appears in sequence: "Art by" receding, "Ayesha" landing, italic gold "Johar" last. Then the line: *Beauty is a form of resistance.* A room you have walked into where something is already happening. A video layer plays softly beneath the overlay.

**II. The Work.** Five bodies of meaning in an asymmetric grid. The Sacred spans the full left column, the largest canvas, for the work you most want a new visitor to meet first. Each painting opens into a detail view built for close looking.

**III. Who you are.** Dark ink section. Your own description of yourself on one side. On the other, the name: your statement on what Johar means, and your words on beauty as resistance. This section is now in your voice throughout.

**IV. The door.** Original paintings, prints, commissions. Framed as continuation: you have been moved, now here is how you bring this into your life, or further into the world.

## Emotional destination

### What someone should carry away

In version 1 we proposed reflection on the state of the world, then hard-won hope. You told us hope is part of it, but not the heart of it.

The destination is this:

**To leave someone seeing through eyes they never knew they had.**

Colours they never knew they saw. Shapes that could reshape everything around them. Beauty in something uncomfortable. And, through that altered way of seeing, the will to reveal and challenge what is harmful.

This is the brief for the entire experience. Every design decision is held against it. It is why the site rewards looking longer, why the paintings lead on colour, and why the line is about beauty as resistance rather than beauty as comfort.
