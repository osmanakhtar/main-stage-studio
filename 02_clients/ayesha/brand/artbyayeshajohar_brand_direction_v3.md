## What the site is for

### To sell, and to make you more visible

You told us the main purpose of the site is to sell, and to be a more visible artist. That is a clear brief, and it changes how the site is built more than anything else in this version.

**Selling**

- Every painting shows its status at a glance: original available, sold, or available as a print.
- Anyone who loves a painting is one step away from asking about it or buying it. No hunting for a contact page.
- Prints link straight to your Etsy shop, so you do not have to manage a second shop.
- Commissions have a simple way in: tell us what you have in mind, and Ayesha will be in touch.
- "Collect" is always in the navigation, not only at the bottom of the page.

**Being visible**

- Each painting has its own page, with its title, size, medium and story. Those pages can be shared on their own and found through search.
- The site is written so that people searching for a Mauritian-born, London-based painter, or for the themes in your work, can find you.
- Your social channels sit alongside the work, so a visitor can keep following you after they leave.
- A short sign-up lets people hear when new work is available. This is often the difference between someone who admires a painting and someone who buys the next one.
- Exhibitions, press and community work have a place to go as they come, so the site grows with you.

Selling and the experience are not in tension. The calmer and more personal the site feels, the more people stay with a painting, and the more likely they are to want it in their life.

## Discovery

### What we found in the work

Before any creative decisions were made, we looked carefully at your paintings. The mandalas, the Ganesha, the water studies, the Mansa Forts, the George Floyd portrait, the pencil drawing of your wife, the landscapes, the architectural pieces.

You paint what you believe in. Not only what you observe, but what you hold.

The range of your work, which might at first look like stylistic variation, is coherence at a deeper level. The mandala and the George Floyd portrait are not different modes. Both honour what should be inviolable.

Your answers gave us the other half of the through-line. The work is not only about the sacred and the violated. It is about **what is hidden in both**. Colours and shapes exist everywhere, unseen, and your paintings expose them. That is why the same work can hold beauty and discomfort at once, and why it is layered on purpose: the people who love your paintings say there is always something new to see.

And running through all of it, quieter than the rest: love. The pencil drawing of your wife sits differently to everything else. More private. More still. It still needs its own room.

## The line

### Beauty is a form of resistance.

You said it yourself:

> For me, beauty is a form of resistance. Hidden colours and shapes exist everywhere, and once I expose them in my paintings, it forces us to see beauty in both the good and the bad. Through that altered way of seeing, I urge the audience to see what we can reveal, and to challenge what is harmful in a different way.

We recommend this sentence as the line. It appears once on the site, at the arrival, and never again. Its power is in its singularity. The full statement lives in Who I am, where there is room for it.

Your answer about how the site should feel changes how the line is delivered, not what it says. It arrives quietly, set calmly beneath your name and a painting in full colour. The resistance is in the words and the work. The site does not need to raise its voice.

## How it should feel

### First, calm. Then, a new way of seeing.

You told us that when someone lands on your website, they should feel **calm, like they are seeing something original, like they can relate to it.**

Version 2 described only where a visitor ends up. Your answer gives us where they begin. Together they make two steps:

1. **On arrival: calm, original, relatable.** Nothing rushes or confronts. The first thing a visitor meets is a painting, given space, and a person they can connect with. Original, because nothing about it looks like any other artist's site. Relatable, because the words are plain and warm, and the person behind the work is present from the start.
2. **As they look longer: seeing through eyes they never knew they had.** Colours they never knew they saw. Shapes that could reshape everything around them. Beauty in something uncomfortable. And, through that altered way of seeing, the will to reveal and challenge what is harmful.

Calm is not the opposite of resistance here. It is what lets someone stay long enough to see what the paintings are doing.

**What this means for the design**

- The arrival is still and light. No moving video beneath the name, nothing that competes with the painting.
- Words throughout the site are plain and warm. No gallery jargon.
- Witness, the most difficult body of work, is reached through the site rather than met at the door.
- Detail views and slow reveals stay, because they are how the second step happens.

## Visual direction

### Colourful, elegant, sophisticated, approachable

In your words, the site should be **colourful, elegant, sophisticated, and at the same time approachable.**

Version 2 held the site back so that only the paintings carried colour. Your answer asks for more than that. The site should feel colourful in its own right, while staying elegant enough that the paintings still lead. Each of your four words now has a job:

- **Colourful.** Each body of work brings its own colour into the site: in its section, its labels and its painting pages. A visitor moving through the work moves through colour.
- **Elegant.** One idea per screen, generous space, nothing crowding a painting.
- **Sophisticated.** Considered type, careful detail, a craft to the small things. Never flashy.
- **Approachable.** Light, warm surfaces instead of large dark sections. Plain words. Prices and buttons that are easy to find.

### Colour

The identity colours stay, because each one has a direct source in your paintings. What changes is how they are used: warm canvas is the main surface, and the colours appear more often and more confidently, as accents, section colours and backgrounds to individual paintings. The deep teal and ink are used in smaller doses, so the site never feels heavy.

On small text, saffron and gold are used in slightly deeper shades on light backgrounds, and slightly lighter shades on dark ones, so every word stays easy to read.

### Typography

Two typefaces, chosen to feel true to your practice.

- **Fraunces** (display, headlines, the name). A warm serif with the quality of something made rather than designed. The italic "Johar" in gold is the signature moment of the identity.
- **DM Sans** (body, navigation, captions, prices). Clean, warm and unpretentious. It keeps the site approachable.

### Atmosphere: calm, with depth

- **Calm arrival.** Still, light, one painting in full colour.
- **Detail views.** Every painting can be opened large and explored close up, so a visitor can find the colours and shapes that do not show at thumbnail size.
- **Slow reveals.** Details and passages of your own words surface as a visitor moves through the site, rather than all at once.
- **Room to look.** Generous space around each painting. Nothing crowds the work or hurries the eye.
- **Something to come back for.** New work, and a reason to return.

## Site structure

### Four movements, and a page for every painting

The site moves from calm arrival, through the work, to you, to collecting. Collecting is also reachable from anywhere.

**I. Arrival.** A light, still opening. One of your paintings in full colour. The name in sequence: "Art by" receding, "Ayesha" landing, italic gold "Johar" last. Then the line: *Beauty is a form of resistance.* A clear way into the work, and a quiet way to collect.

**II. The Work.** The five bodies of meaning, each in its own colour. Every painting shows its status, and opens to its own page: large detail view, title, size, medium, its story in a sentence or two, and how to buy the original or a print.

**III. Who I am.** Your paragraph, your statement on Johar, your words on beauty as resistance, and a photograph of you. Light and warm, so it feels like meeting you.

**IV. Collect.** Originals, prints (through Etsy), commissions, and a sign-up to hear about new work first. Your social channels sit here too, so people can keep following you.
